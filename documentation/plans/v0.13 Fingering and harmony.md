# v0.13: fingering and harmony

Goal: three small features for learners, in one release, then the Music Practice dependency bump.

## Both hands' scale fingering (#49)

- The standard fingerings exist since #68 (`fingering: auto`, `auto left`: major, natural, harmonic and melodic minor).
- New: `fingering: auto both` on a keyboard scale draws two diagrams in a column: the right hand (two octaves from the tonic), then the left hand an octave lower, each titled "<scale>, right hand" / "<scale>, left hand" and playable on its own. With `range`, both keyboards use it.
- Other scales keep the existing message (write the fingers).

## Fingering in plain words (#53)

- `caption: fingering` on a `chord` block writes the fingering in words in an HTML paragraph under the diagram (not inside the SVG): it wraps, screen readers and search read it (a step toward #14).
- Guitar: the strings to strum ("Strum from the 5th string (A)."), muted inner strings, each finger with its string and fret ("Index on the D string, 2nd fret."), barres ("Index bars strings 1 to 6 at the 1st fret."), the thumb. String names follow the tuning: standard names on a standard guitar, note names otherwise.
- Piano: each hand's fingers on their keys ("Right hand: thumb on C, middle finger on E, little finger on G."), from the fingering the block works out or the one written.
- Works in rows (`---`, `voicings:`), as a shared key. API: `fingeringInWords(...)` for apps.

## Harmonize any scale (#43)

- `harmonize(scale, { kind })`: the chord on each degree of any 7-note scale (major, natural minor, the modes, harmonic minor, melodic minor), with its numeral. Kinds: `triads`, `sevenths`, `sus` (sus4, or sus2 where the 4th is not in the scale; degrees with neither are left out and said), `power` (degrees without a perfect fifth are left out and said). Chords are stacked from the scale's own notes, spelled for the key.
- Block: a `progression` block without a chords line, with `key:` (any 7-note scale) and `harmonize: triads | sevenths | sus | power`, draws the seven chords with numerals, diagrams (guitar or piano) and the play bar, like any progression.
