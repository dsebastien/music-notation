## Easier chords and the best capo (#45, first part: the functions)

- Asked by Sébastien at the end of the day, with Music Practice #68. He chose two things from examples: a chord changes only when a plainer chord of its family is easier to play (not always down to the basic chord), and the best capo shows through the chart's `{capo: auto}` and the plugin's command, not as a second switch on the bar.
- `src/chord/simplify.ts`: `simplifyChord`, `easierChord`, `capoChoices`, `bestCapo`, exported from the main entry, with `simplify.spec.ts` (15).
- Decisions:
    - "Easier" is the difficulty of the easiest voicing the generator finds. So Am7 stays (x02010 is easier than Am), Cmaj9 becomes Cmaj7 and not C, and G5 becomes G.
    - The family is read from the chord's intervals, not from a table of suffixes, so every chord the parser reads has one.
    - A capo fret costs half a point of difficulty when two frets give as many open shapes. Without it, Eb Bb Cm Ab came out at capo 6 (A E F#m D) and C#m A E B at capo 7.
    - The number of open shapes still comes first, as the ticket says: C G Am F is capo 5 (G D Em C), because F is a barre.
- Measured: 36 chords simplified in 133 ms, 8 songs ranked in 15 ms (the generator's results are kept).
