## #33 root for note lists

- `root:` for note lists (`notes:` and list shorthand lines) on fretboards and keyboards: `labels: intervals` writes each note's interval from the root (R, b3, 3, #4, b7, through `intervalLabel`), `labels: degrees` the interval number (1 3 5, like chord degrees), the root gets its color when it's in the list (it needn't be: interval maps). Exact pitches (`notes: A3 C4 E4`) work too; `highlight:` picks a rooted list's intervals; `compare:` with a type takes the root.
- Spelling: tonal's `Interval.distance` on the letters (D# above C is #2, Gb is b5); the root's pitch class is R (B# above C too); a unison or octave that isn't perfect takes the semitone's usual name (C# above C is b2, Cb is 7). The list keeps its own names. The root may be written in any case, with ♯/♭ or an octave (ignored).
- A rooted list stays a list: plays one note at a time on keyboards (not as a chord), no fretboard positions, no keyboard fingering (`NoteSet.list`). With `root:`, a shorthand line is read as a list first (`E` with `root: C` is one note, not E major).
- Errors: `root:` with a scale or a chord (they have their own root), a root that isn't a note name (`Invalid root "H": use a note name, e.g. root: C`), a root with no notes. Without `root:` lists are unchanged. `listNoteSet(text, root?)`; `intervalFrom`, `rootNameOf` in `note-set.ts`.

## #34 positions in neck order

- `positions: all` follows the neck: position 1 in its lowest octave, each next one in the lowest octave whose lowest fret is at or above the previous one's, back to its own lowest octave past fret 36 (`MAX_FRET`). Boxes, CAGED and 3NPS alike. A minor pentatonic: 5-8, 7-10, 9-13, 12-15, 14-17 (was 5-8, 7-10, 9-13, 0-3, 2-5). A list (`positions: 4 5`) keeps the frets `all` gives them.
- The row passes each diagram its frets: a single position with `frets:` is drawn in the lowest octave inside them, so every diagram of the row can be written as its own block. Without `frets:`, a single `position: n` keeps its lowest octave. The block's own `frets:` is dropped from the row (it was already ignored).
- `numbering: minor` (or `relative minor`) counts a major pentatonic's boxes and 3NPS positions from its relative minor: A major pentatonic's position 1 is F# minor's box 1 (2-5), then 4-7, 6-10, 9-12, 11-14 (default numbering: 4-7, 6-10, 9-12, 11-14, 14-17; was 4-7, 6-10, 9-12, 11-14, 2-5). Row titles add "(numbered from F# minor)". An error on other scales, on CAGED shapes and without a position. Not offered for major scale CAGED shapes: they are named by letter, and their numbers follow the CAGED order from the E shape.
- API: `neckPositions`, `scalePosition`'s `within` and `numbering` options, `MAX_FRET`, the `Numbering` and `PositionOptions` types.

## Text tabs: bars split across rows (#70)

- `textTabToAlphaTex` joins bars split across rows. A typical bar's width is the median (the upper one) of the bars with notes and a bar line on each side; with fewer than two such bars nothing is joined. A fragment is a bar 2/3 of that width at most.
- A row's last bar, a fragment, joins the next row's first bar when no bar line ends the row (`|----` in Nothing Else Matters), or when that bar is a fragment too and no bar line starts its row, or the two are at most a quarter wider than a typical bar (bar lines on both sides). The joined bar's durations come from its own width; the next row's dashes before beat 1 don't count. A fragment without notes after the last bar line (`|--`) is left out.
- A fragment before a row's first bar line (`e ----0-2-|3---`) joins the bar before it when that bar is a fragment; else it is a bar's end (rests first). The tab's first bar, a fragment, is a pickup bar (`\ac`, its notes only). The tab's last bar, a fragment without a closing bar line, is a bar's start (rests last). Both are placed in a bar of the typical width, so their notes keep their lengths. Other short bars stay 4/4 bars of their own width.
- Nothing Else Matters intro (UG 54340): bar 4 was `:2 0.6 0.3` (a 4-column fragment), now `8 8 8 8 8 8 r4`, the same as bars 1–3. The row 3 → row 4 join (4 + 12 columns, the tab's spacing uneven there) comes out as one bar of eighths and sixteenths.

## Text tabs: the meter from the typical bar

- `textTabToAlphaTex` no longer assumes 4/4. The typical bars (a bar line on each side, notes, the median width a column more or less) vote: their unit is the narrowest gap between beats at least half as frequent as the most frequent gap (a shuffle's eighths); a bar holds as many units as fit its width (half a unit left over is none) or up to its last beat. When 2/3 of them (two at least), their beats on the units, hold 3 units: `\ts 3 4`; 12: `\ts 12 8`; 6: `\ts 6 8`, or `\ts 3 4` when more bars group the six in twos (beats only on units 0 2 4, or bass notes, the lowest string when it doesn't sound on every beat, so) than in threes. Six even eighths with no grouping (Nothing Else Matters) default to 6/8. Any other count, or no agreement: 4/4, as before.
- Bars, grids and rests follow the meter: bar length 12 sixteenths in 3/4 and 6/8, 24 in 12/8; grids 3/4 dotted half, quarters, eighths, sixteenths; 6/8 dotted half, dotted quarters, eighths, sixteenths; 12/8 dotted whole, dotted halves, dotted quarters, eighths, sixteenths. Rests show the beats (r8 r4. in 6/8, a dotted half rest on beat 1 or 3 of 12/8); an empty bar is one rest of the bar (a dotted half in 6/8). Joined bars, pickups and fragments use the same meter.
- Nothing Else Matters intro: `\ts 6 8`, bars 1–4 six eighths with no rest. The tab's 13-column bars hold seven eighths, more than a 6/8 bar; they fall back to sixteenths and rests (in 4/4 they were seven eighths and an eighth rest).
