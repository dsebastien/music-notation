## #33 root for note lists

- `root:` for note lists (`notes:` and list shorthand lines) on fretboards and keyboards: `labels: intervals` writes each note's interval from the root (R, b3, 3, #4, b7, through `intervalLabel`), `labels: degrees` the interval number (1 3 5, like chord degrees), the root gets its color when it's in the list (it needn't be: interval maps). Exact pitches (`notes: A3 C4 E4`) work too; `highlight:` picks a rooted list's intervals; `compare:` with a type takes the root.
- Spelling: tonal's `Interval.distance` on the letters (D# above C is #2, Gb is b5); the root's pitch class is R (B# above C too); a unison or octave that isn't perfect takes the semitone's usual name (C# above C is b2, Cb is 7). The list keeps its own names. The root may be written in any case, with ♯/♭ or an octave (ignored).
- A rooted list stays a list: plays one note at a time on keyboards (not as a chord), no fretboard positions, no keyboard fingering (`NoteSet.list`). With `root:`, a shorthand line is read as a list first (`E` with `root: C` is one note, not E major).
- Errors: `root:` with a scale or a chord (they have their own root), a root that isn't a note name (`Invalid root "H": use a note name, e.g. root: C`), a root with no notes. Without `root:` lists are unchanged. `listNoteSet(text, root?)`; `intervalFrom`, `rootNameOf` in `note-set.ts`.
- Business Rules (note sets), the guide (keyboard and fretboard key tables, a live fretboard example), the API page.
- Not checked in a real Obsidian window or browser: covered by happy-dom tests only.

## #34 positions in neck order

- `positions: all` follows the neck: position 1 in its lowest octave, each next one in the lowest octave whose lowest fret is at or above the previous one's, back to its own lowest octave past fret 36 (`MAX_FRET`). Boxes, CAGED and 3NPS alike. A minor pentatonic: 5-8, 7-10, 9-13, 12-15, 14-17 (was 5-8, 7-10, 9-13, 0-3, 2-5). A list (`positions: 4 5`) keeps the frets `all` gives them.
- The row passes each diagram its frets: a single position with `frets:` is drawn in the lowest octave inside them, so every diagram of the row can be written as its own block. Without `frets:`, a single `position: n` keeps its lowest octave. The block's own `frets:` is dropped from the row (it was already ignored).
- `numbering: minor` (or `relative minor`) counts a major pentatonic's boxes and 3NPS positions from its relative minor: A major pentatonic's position 1 is F# minor's box 1 (2-5), then 4-7, 6-10, 9-12, 11-14 (default numbering: 4-7, 6-10, 9-12, 11-14, 14-17; was 4-7, 6-10, 9-12, 11-14, 2-5). Row titles add "(numbered from F# minor)". An error on other scales, on CAGED shapes and without a position. Not offered for major scale CAGED shapes: they are named by letter, and their numbers follow the CAGED order from the E shape.
- API: `neckPositions`, `scalePosition`'s `within` and `numbering` options, `MAX_FRET`, the `Numbering` and `PositionOptions` types.
- Not checked in Obsidian or a browser: the guide's new live examples load the released 0.5 bundle and show the new layout only after a release.

## Follow the notes (#41)

- While a chord, keyboard or fretboard plays, the sounding note's `data-midi` element gets `mn-playing` for the length of its note, on the drawing on the page (a redrawing for the other sound too). Scales light every place of a pitch; a strummed chord lights its strings low to high, one each when strings share a pitch (`A5 50xxxx`); a clicked note lights only what was clicked. `src/playback/follow.ts`: `noteTargets`, `followNotes` (one timer at a time, read against the clock; ends after the last note, on Stop, on a sound switch, and when the drawing leaves the page).
- Timing: `NotePlayer.play` options gain an optional `onStart` (the notes really sound), and players an optional `reportsStart`. The light synthesizer calls it at its start time on the audio clock (after the context resumes, plus the output latency); `soundfontPlayer` right after the synthesizer starts; `createSoundfontPlayer` once the soundfont is in (its fallback reports itself, or starts at once when it doesn't). The bar waits for `onStart` from reporting players (or players seen calling it); others light from the play call after 300 ms, resynced by a later `onStart`. Backward compatible: existing players and wrappers work unchanged.
- Styles: lit notes are yellow (#facc15) with a ring in the text color and dark labels, over root, characteristic and highlighted colors. Tabs and scores: alphaTab ships no style for its cursor or beat highlight, so neither showed; the bar cursor, beat line, sounding notes and selection now use `--mn-accent` (the player bar's).
- Checked: tsc, lint, tests (fake clock for the follower, a stub AudioContext for the light player's `onStart`, bar tests for Stop, sound switch, redrawn diagrams, clicks, removed diagrams, non-reporting players), build. Not checked in Obsidian or a browser: the lit colors in light and dark themes, the sync with the soundfont's first play, and alphaTab's cursor and highlight colors.

## Text tab converter (#37)

- `textTabToAlphaTex` reads more layouts from the web: drum tabs (`HH|x-x-…`, `Snare |`, `BD|o---`, names in letters or words) as a percussion track with alphaTab's default articulations (`\instrument percussion`, `\articulation defaults`, names such as `HiHatClosed`, `SnareHit`, `KickHit`); strings numbered `1|` as standard tuning; one named line or more, three unnamed lines (a guitar's top three); text after the last bar line (`| let ring`, `| x2`) as a `{txt}` annotation on the system's last beat; systems written without a blank line between them.
- Blank staves become empty bars (a whole rest each) rather than null, so a stave to fill still draws with its strings; a bar of dashes only between others is a rest bar too (it was dropped).
- Octaves: names that match consecutive strings of a standard guitar keep guitar octaves (`e B G D` → E4 B3 G3 D3, was E3 B2 G2 D2); only E A D G and B E A D G go an octave down. Flat names (half a step down) are read.
- `tab` blocks with a percussion track draw notation too (Tab profile → ScoreTab): alphaTab draws no tablature for drums, so they drew nothing.
- Checked: every sample (in the spec) loads with alphaTab's `ScoreLoader.loadAlphaTex`, with the expected GM drum numbers, tunings, bars and beat texts; long adversarial lines run in milliseconds; the chord-sheet tests pass unchanged. ## ChordPro chord definitions and chords used (#10)

- `{define}` and `{chord}` in ChordPro 6 syntax (`chords/defines.ts`): `base-fret`, frets relative to it (`x`, `N`, `-1` muted), fingers (1–9, A–Z), `keys` (semitones above the root), `display`, `copy` / `copyall` (an earlier define, else the voicing lookup), `diagram off`. `{chord: Am}` draws in place (the song's define so far, else the lookup), transposed only in brackets, as ChordPro says. Mistakes show at the top of the chart and make the block not ok (so `check` reports them); the chart still draws.
- Chords used row (`chords/chart-diagrams.ts`): `{diagrams}` / `{diagrams: on|top|bottom|off}` (`right` → top, `below` → bottom), or the new `chartDiagrams` render option. Off by default, so existing notes don't change; `on` means top (where songbooks put it; ChordPro's default is bottom). Lyrics' chords by first appearance, as printed (transposed), at most 32; defines first (by name, else by spelling), then the voicing lookup (guitar) or root position from C4 (piano). Reuses the row CSS (`mn-row` / `mn-cell`, wraps). Undrawable chords are listed under the row with the `{define}` to write.
- Transpose: a define with no open string moves (down when its lowest fret stays ≥ 1, else up, at most fret 15); a define with open strings gives way to the lookup (an open G moved two frets is not a playable A); keys move with the root. Capo: the shapes as played plus a "Capo N" caption (guitar only); no capo bar in the diagrams (#6 does that).
- Instrument: `{instrument: piano}` (an extension other ChordPro tools ignore), else `instrument` option, else the note's tags (`renderBlock` resolves `properties` for `chords` blocks), else guitar. On Publish non-empty blocks get no properties, so only the directive picks piano there.
- `pianoPitches` moved from `blocks/renderers.ts` to `keyboard/piano-chord.ts` (shared). Exports: `chordsUsed`, `parseDefinition`, `ChordDefinition`; `Song` gained `definitions`, `diagrams`, `instrument`, `warnings`; `Line` gained `{ kind: 'chord' }`.
- Checked: tsc, lint, tests (happy-dom), build. Not checked in Obsidian or a browser: the row in light and dark themes, wrapping on a narrow pane, the play bars on row diagrams, Publish (the guide's live example needs a release).

## Strumming patterns (#8, #22)

- #8: a `strum` block (new fence) draws a pattern on a beat grid: counts (1 & 2 &, 1 e & a, 1 & a, 1 2 3 4 5 6 in 6/8), an arrow per strum, accents (`D>` or `>D`), muted strums (`X`, drawn as an X), ghost strums (`(U)`, small and in parentheses), chord names over the slot they start on, bar lines; rows of up to 16 slots, at most 16 bars; a long title or chord name widens the drawing. Keys: `time`, `subdivision` (8ths, 16ths, triplets), `chord`, `tempo`/`bpm`, `tuning`, `title`, `pattern`.
- Written forms: one symbol a slot with rests (`D - D U - U D U`, `D-DU-UDU`, `D . D U`); strums alone placed by the hand's motion, down on the beat and up between (`D DU UDU`, `DDUUDU`, `D D D D`; triplets down-up-down each beat); dashes alternating with strums read as separators (`D-D-U-U-D-U`); each line and each part between `|` read on its own. A chord line above a pattern line (each chord on the slot under it, or on the rest the motion put in the gap under it), or `[G]` inline (words too). Count lines are skipped. A line of only D's reads as strums. Errors: unknown symbol (lists the symbols), wrong count (slots a bar, and which other subdivision would fit), unknown chord (suggestions), unclosed `( [`, accent on a rest or a ghost, chord past the end, unknown key, triplets outside x/4, tempo outside 20–240.
- Each slot is a `g[data-mn-beat]` (index from 0) with `data-mn-stroke` (`down`, `up`, `mute`, `rest`), accent and ghost flags and an invisible `rect.mn-strum-cell`, for the highlight stream (#41). The SVG holds `data-mn-strum` (the pattern written back), time, subdivision, tempo and the notes of each chord (`data-mn-voicing`). `check --describe` prints strum blocks.
- #22: `playStrum(pattern, { bpm, chord, tuning, countIn, loop, player, window, … })`, `strumEvents`, `scoreOf`, `strumVoicing`. Down strums play the chord's strings low to high, up strums the 4 highest high to low (about 12 ms apart, closer at fast tempos); velocities 90, accent 120, ghost 40, muted 80 for 40 ms; a strum rings until the next one. Chords: the `voicings` lookup on a standard guitar (what chord diagrams show), else the easiest generated voicing for the tuning; without a chord, E minor (open strings on other tunings); strums before the first change take `chord:`, else the last change (the pattern repeats).
- Loop without a gap: each pass is scheduled by the player like the first, started on a timer at the previous pass's exact end (counted from the first start, so delays don't add up); a looping pass has no ring past its end. Count-in: one bar of high clicks, first pass only. Tempo counts the time signature's beat (an 8th in 6/8).
- The play bar on strum blocks: play/stop, loop, count-in, tempo (BPM 20–240; the block's tempo, else the app's `bpm`, else 90), volume, sound; changes apply from the next pass; a loop stops when the drawing leaves the page; the window is read at each play (popouts).
- `NoteEvent.velocity` (optional, 1–127, 100 when absent): the light synthesizer scales each note's envelope by velocity/100; the soundfont player passes it to alphaTab's `addNote` (clamped 1–127).
- Docs: guide section, API, README, the Publish loader (`strum` added to the languages that load the bundle: Publish sites must update their `publish.js`), playground, live-examples script.
- Checked: tests (parser forms and errors, drawing, describe, events, looping with hand-run timers, the bar, both players' velocity); the check CLI on strum blocks (linkedom); the Publish bundle in headless Chrome (blocks drawn, sources hidden, play/loop/count-in/stop run without errors); screenshots in light and dark page colors.
