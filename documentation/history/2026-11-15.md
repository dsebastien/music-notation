## Full screen for tabs and scores (#113)

- Asked by Sébastien after a review of web tab players: in full screen the score takes the whole width of an ultrawide screen, on several lines, and scrolls.
- `src/tab/full-screen.ts` (`fullScreenControl`, no alphaTab in it: `follow(on)` is the caller's) and its spec; wired in `renderTab` (`fullScreen`, default true); icons `full` and `exit` in the bar parts; styles `mn-fullscreen`.
- The score follows the music only in full screen: `scrollElement` is the block's surface from the start, `scrollMode` `OffScreen` there and `Off` elsewhere. #89 holds: out of full screen nothing scrolls.
- Checked in Chrome (a local page with the built publish and tab bundles, a 64-bar tab in a 700 px column, viewport 1462 x 726). The click was automated, so the browser refused its own full screen and the block covered the window (the fallback):
    - 2 bars a line in the page, 5 in full screen; the score's area scrolled (1,768 px of music in 664 px), the bar at the bottom, the page's background taken.
    - Playing from bar 20: the area scrolled to the next line by itself (scrollTop 624) and the page stayed where it was.
    - A wheel scroll stopped the following: the area stayed where the wheel left it for 7 s of playback.
    - Escape left it: the width, the focus (on the button) and the background came back.
    - Found there: leaving moved the page from 1,513 px to 5,665 px. Cause: the block is fixed in full screen, so the page below moved up, and the browser's scroll anchoring followed that text when the block came back and grew. Fixed with a placeholder of the block's height and a kept `min-height` until the score is drawn again; checked: the page at 4,461 px before, in, just after and 4 s after.
- Not checked: the browser's own full screen (it needs a real click), Obsidian (a popout window, the plugin's dev build), a phone, a 3,440 px or 5,120 px wide screen, the sound.
- Seen during the check, not changed: on a plain web page the tab bar is not sticky at the bottom of the screen. `.mn-block` has `overflow: auto`, which makes the block the bar's scroll container.
- One spec fails on Windows, before and after this change: `src/cli/check.spec.ts`, "files, folders (their Markdown files) and globs", expects forward slashes in paths and gets backslashes.

## Sounds: where the files come from (#124)

- Sébastien: no dependency on a CDN for the library to work; the library should depend on the sounds package instead.
- Decided: the library has no built-in address for the instrument files. Apps install the sounds package and pass its bytes (`assets.instruments` as a function). Web pages name a folder: `play: { instruments: '<folder URL>' }` in `window.musicNotationOptions` (`pageInstruments`, read by the tab bundle). Without it, the soundfont that ships with alphaTab plays, as before; with nothing at all, the light synthesizer.
- The optional peer dependency on the sounds package is not in `package.json` yet: the package is not published, and a peer that doesn't exist breaks the install. To add right after the first publish, as alphaTab's is.

## The sample player's loops: a buzz while a note rang (#128)

- Heard by Sébastien on a page of diagrams: a chord starts clean, then gets a vibration and some noise while it rings.
- Step 1 of the ticket: the bank cut again as SF2 (no compression, 51 MB of program files). Measured in headless Chrome (a test page and a small DevTools-protocol driver, outside the repository): the uncompressed files had no fault, so it came from the compressed ones.
- Cause: Chrome's `decodeAudioData` gives an Ogg Vorbis sample up to about 230 points shorter than it was (8 of the steel guitar's 10 samples: 13,751 points became 13,696, 17,637 became 17,408). A loop ends close to its sample's end (often 20 points before it), so the loop's end was past the buffer, the player clamped it, and the loop jumped from the wrong point at each turn: the jump at the loop was 7 to 16 times a normal step between two points (0.1 to 1.3 in the uncompressed files). The compression itself does not break a loop: where the decoded sample was whole, its loop met as well as the original's.
- Fix, two parts: `withTail` (`scripts/sounds/sf2.ts`) gives each sample 1,024 more points before it is compressed (its loop going on, or silence), so a decoder drops only the tail; `loopWithin` (`src/playback/sampled.ts`) moves a loop back as a whole when a buffer ends before it, for files cut before the fix. The check that plays each compressed sample alone in alphaTab now gives the uncompressed form the same tail (without that, 182 samples failed it, because a sample played alone plays its tail too).
- Measured, held notes of 5 s rendered offline in Chrome, the difference from the same notes played from the uncompressed files, from second 1 to 5: before -3.7 to +3.4 dB (the sound left the original once it looped: G chord +2.2, G4 +3.4, D4 +3.1); after -24.9 to -26.4 dB, the compression's own difference, as on a note the bug didn't touch (A3, -26.2 before and after). The piano drifted less (-20.8 dB in the fifth second before, -33.9 after).
- alphaTab, same notes, old, new and uncompressed files: the same measures in all three. Its own decoder keeps the loop.
- The files cut again: 13.5 MB in all (13.6 MB before: fewer samples needed a higher quality), steel guitar 112 KB (110), grand piano 392 KB (359), median 84 KB (82). 14 samples stay uncompressed (13 before).
- `bun run tsc`, `lint` pass; `bun run test`: 1,276 pass, 1 fail (the Windows one, #123).
- Not checked: Sébastien's ears (the comparison page), Safari and Firefox decoders, a phone. The sounds package still holds the files cut before the fix.
