## Chart chords that last more or less than a bar (#75, branch lib-chartbars)

- Engine: `ProgressionBar.beats` (written beats; a bar when absent; the last chord fills its bar). `progressionEvents` builds a beat timeline: each beat strums the chord sounding then, `bar` strikes at the bar's start and each chord change, an arpeggio starts again on a new chord, a pattern slot plays its beat's chord. A pass's `strums` are one per chord (to light it), its `bars` one per bar. `playProgression` takes `{ chord, pitches?, beats? }`, calls `onBar` per bar (the chord sounding) and the new `onChord` per chord. Without `beats`, every output is as before (the old specs pass unchanged).
- Charts: a lyrics line with a bar line `|` is measured (`lineBeats`): chords between two bar lines share the bar, by dots (`[G5]. . [D5]. [A5].`, the Music wiki's riff notation) or slashes (`[C] / / [G]`) when they add up to the bar's counted beats, else evenly; an empty bar holds the chord before it (across lines); an N.C.-only bar is silent. Lines without `|` keep one chord a bar. Marks carry `data-mn-beats`; the drawing is unchanged. `chartChords(song, { transpose })` exports the same reading for apps (Music Practice #69/#89: use it instead of reading chart chords one a bar).
- Rejected: a duration inside the brackets (`[G*2]`): other ChordPro tools show it as a broken chord. A repeated chord (`[G] [G]`) already plays two bars.
- `expectLinear` moved to `src/test-setup.ts`, shared by the converter's and the chart's linear-time specs.
- Wiki: 41 of the 176 `chords` blocks write bar lines (12-bar blues, riffs, ii-V grids) and now play with those lengths (for example Power Chords' riff `| [G5]. . [D5]. [A5]. |`: 2 + 1 + 1 beats; Seventh Chords' Gmaj7 three bars). `check --describe` is identical to the published 0.11.0 (4772 lines; it doesn't describe chord lengths).
- Checked: tsc, lint, 902 tests, build, wiki check (164 files, 765 blocks, 763 ok, 0 errors, 2 skipped). Not checked by ear or in an Obsidian window.

## Diagram titles that wrap (#74) and touch tap targets (#73 item 5), branch lib-size

- #74: a long title (the #72 names, a host's "9. " prefix, wiki titles such as "G shape, sounds Gb (F#)") widened a chord diagram or a fretboard; a host that draws them at a fixed width (Music Practice's voicing grid) scaled the whole drawing down. Now the title wraps, never widens (rule "Diagram titles"). First tried: a smaller font then `textLength` on one line, to keep the height; rejected after measuring the wiki: 108 of 291 blocks' titles would have been squeezed, "Mandolin G chop chord, G/D with D in the bass (G D A E tuning)" into 98 units. Wrapping instead: one title squeezed (that one, on its third line). `titleWidth` (8 for a space) broke "F#m7b5, Drop 3 (strings 2-4, 6)" into three lines inside "Drop 3"; `titleLineWidth` estimates closer and breaks after commas and before parentheses first: "F#m7b5, Drop 3" / "(strings 2-4, 6)".
- Rows: every chord diagram and fretboard of a block gets the room of its longest title (`alignTitleLines`), so nuts line up in `voicings:`, `---` rows, `positions: all` and chart diagram rows. Exported for apps (Music Practice #87): `alignTitleLines`, `titleLines`, `titleLineWidth`, `MAX_TITLE_LINES`, `TITLE_LINE`.
- Wiki, drawn with 0.11.0 and with the branch (291 chord and fretboard blocks): 218 diagrams are narrower (they had been widened by their title), 203 have two or three title lines or the room of them, 1 is squeezed. `check --describe` is identical to 0.11.0 (it reads the title's text, which wrapping keeps).
- #73 item 5: on a coarse pointer, chord diagrams and fretboards are drawn a third larger (`zoom: 1.34`), so a string's lane (18 units on chord diagrams, 20 on fretboards) is at least 24 px. CSS only (no inline style: a strict CSP on a Publish site would block style attributes). #15 (a size option) is not needed for this: the hit areas already span the whole lane, and only the drawn size was too small on touch screens.
- Specs: the title rules (one line, wrap, squeeze, row alignment, exports) and the coarse-pointer rule, each failing before; "makes room for a long title" changed on purpose (the diagram keeps its width). Checks: tsc, lint, 896 tests, build, wiki check (765 blocks, 763 ok, 0 errors, 2 skipped).
- Not checked here: the real text widths in Obsidian (the estimate decides the line breaks; a line wider than estimated would be clipped at the diagram's edge), and the zoom on a phone (Obsidian mobile, Safari, Firefox 126+ support `zoom`).

## Fix: a bar of N.C. keeps its time (#75)

- Found in the docs review: the rule said a measured bar holding only N.C. is silent, but the code skipped it, so `| [G] | [N.C.] | [Em] |` played G then Em at once and the chart ran a bar early.
- `lineBeats` returns a `rest` step for it (its N.C. segment); the chart marks that N.C. as a bar (`data-mn-bar`, `data-mn-rest`, `data-mn-beats`), which the play bar plays as silence and lights; `chartChords` gives `{ chord: null, pitches: [], beats }`. In a bar with chords that play, N.C. still takes no time; lines without bar lines still leave N.C. out. A trailing N.C. bar is now a bar of silence (spec updated on purpose).

## Harmonize any scale (#43, branch lib-harmony)

- `harmonize(scale, { kind })` in `src/theory/harmonize.ts`, and `readHarmonyScale(text)` (a scale or a reason in words): any 7-note scale, a key through `readKey` (major, minor, modes) or a 7-note scale tonal knows (harmonic and melodic minor, phrygian dominant, …). Chords come from the scale's own notes (`stackedChord`), not tonal's key chords: tonal gives Am6 on the melodic minor's tonic, the stack gives AmMaj7.
- Numerals: `numeralOf(..., { byDegree: true })` in the key the scale is written in (the key itself for modes; else the major or minor key of its third), chosen so every numeral reads back to its chord with `readNumeral` (a spec checks it for four scales and four kinds). So the melodic minor's 6th degree is `#viø7` (a minor key's unmarked vi is the natural 6th), while the raised 7th's diminished chords stay `vii°`/`viiø7` (the leading tone). The minor-major seventh shows as `imaj7` and the augmented major seventh as `bIIImaj7#5`, as the numerals module already writes them (kept: changing `numeralQuality` would change existing outputs).
- Functions: `harmonicFunction` would read the melodic minor's D7 and F#m7b5 in A minor as secondary dominants; `harmonize` keeps tonic, subdominant and dominant from it and otherwise takes the function by degree (minor T S T S D S D).
- Kinds: `sus` (sus4 when the 4th is perfect, else sus2 when the 2nd is major) and `power`; both need a perfect fifth, the degrees without one are listed in `skipped` and under the block's key line ("Left out: degree 5 (B), no perfect fifth", `.mn-progression-note`).
- Block: `progression` with `harmonize:` and `key:` (any 7-note scale; without `harmonize:` modes are still refused), no chords line; `transpose:` moves the tonic as its major or minor key would; `harmonize:` may be shared by `---` sections. The section reuses the progression renderer (diagrams, numerals, Nashville, functions, piano voicings, play bar).
- Specs failed first: 12 in `harmonize.spec.ts`, 7 in `progression.spec.ts`. Checks: tsc, lint, 946 tests, build, wiki check (164 files, 765 blocks, 763 ok, 0 errors, 2 skipped), `check --describe` of the wiki identical to the published 0.12.0 (4772 lines). `check --describe` on harmonized blocks (A melodic minor sevenths, D dorian sus on piano, E phrygian power, G# harmonic minor sevenths with F##dim7, C phrygian dominant with voice leading): every diagram draws. Not checked in an Obsidian window or by ear.
