# Business Rules

- **Block languages**: `chord`, `chords`, `keyboard`, `fretboard` (0.1); `tab` and its alias `score` (0.5); `strum`, `progression`, `circle` (0.8); `picking` (0.11). Fence names are stable API: never rename one.
- **Block syntax**: `key: value` lines, keys case-insensitive, last value wins; an optional first line without a key is the block's shorthand. Any other line without a key is an error, except in `strum` (pattern lines) and `progression` (chord lines, anywhere among the keys).
- **Empty blocks** (only whitespace) render nothing, successfully: templates scaffold empty blocks.
- **Errors**: a block with a mistake renders an inline message (what's wrong and what to write) and never throws. On Publish its source stays visible.
- **Publish**: blocks are rendered once (`data-mp-rendered` on the `<pre>`: `pending`, `done`, `error`); the source `<pre>` is hidden only after a successful render; the page is watched (MutationObserver, one pass per frame) because Publish swaps content as you navigate. The tab bundle loads alphaTab's script from jsDelivr once, with an AMD `define` on the page hidden while it runs (alphaTab's UMD build would register with it instead of setting `window.alphaTab`), and forgets a load that fails, a network error or a script that sets no `window.alphaTab` (a blocked or partial download), so the next page change tries again (#38; before, only a network error was forgotten, and every later change failed at once); `alphaTabLoader` in `src/tab/load.ts`.
- **Chord symbols**: `♯`/`♭` are read as `#`/`b`; a slash starts a bass only when a note name follows (`C6/9` has no bass). Suffix spellings are mapped to one canonical form (`theory/suffix.ts`: Δ7/M7 → maj7, min6 → m6, ø7 → m7b5, m(maj7) → mMaj7, alt → 7alt…) before tonal and the voicing data see them, so every symbol the parser accepts also draws. Alterations and additions in whole groups of parentheses read like the same symbol without them (`C7(b9)` = C7b9, `C7(b5,#9)` = C7b5#9, `Cm(add9)` = Cmadd9), for the quality name too, after the written suffix (`C-(maj7)` keeps its own name); unbalanced or empty parentheses and commas outside a group are not a chord (`C,`, `C()`); the root and the type reach tonal apart, so a group starting with an accidental never changes the root (`C(#5)` = Caug, `C(b9)` is no chord, not C♭9); chord-sheet spellings tonal knows under another name are mapped (#5 → aug; -5 and (b5) → Mb5 "Major flat 5", maj7b5 → M7b5, mmaj9 → mMaj9, m6/9 and m6add9 → m69 "Minor 6/9", sus2sus4 → sus24). A flat root stays a root: `Cb5` is a C♭ power chord. Roots and basses take a double sharp (`##` or `x`, read as `##`; a root's `x` before a digit is a chord sheet's count, `Ax2`) or a double flat (`bb`), the longest accidental first (`F##dim` is vii° in G# minor; `Cbb5` is a C𝄫 power chord); their names say "Double Sharp" and "Double Flat", and read back. `parseChord` accepts any other chord tonal knows, with its suffix as the quality.
- **Voicing lookup** (`voicings` entry): a symbol's n-th voicing from chords-db; canonical suffixes reach it under its own names (major, minor, mmaj7, mmaj9, maj7b5, alt, 69); a chord it doesn't have (Cmaj9#11, C7b5#9, Csus2sus4…) gets the generator's 4 easiest voicings, cached per symbol (the last 64) and handed out as copies (a block's position changes its base fret). 7#5 and 9#5 reach the database's aug7 and aug9. Checked lists (#62, `voicings/check.ts`, cached per chord): the curated standard shapes (`voicings/curated.ts`: our own list, e.g. Cmaj7 x32000 fingered 3-2, Dm7 xx0211 2-1-1, the Hendrix grip for 7#9 and 7alt, 3-string power chords, Ab 466544) come first, then the database's voicings that play the chord (every required tone as the generator counts them, no other note, the root or slash bass lowest (any chord note for a dim7, which is symmetrical: Cdim7 xx1212), no pitch on two strings, a hand can finger it; 7alt: root, 3, b7 and at least one of b5 #5 b9 #9); one with a wrong bass or note loses its lowest strings while four stay (two for power chords: B 224442 → x24442), else it's dropped; wrong fingers (a finger on two frets, a middle barre, a pinky barre other than on the two highest strings, fingers crossing) are worked out again; duplicates are dropped; the generator's easiest fill the list up to the database's count (4 at least); then the voicings you can strum (`strummable`: no muted string inside the shape, except the A string muted under a low-E root, the jazz grips F#6 2x132x, Fm6 1x0111) and the curated ones come first, the others after them (Bdim starts with x2343x, not x20x01) (#72). Suffixes tonal doesn't read (aug9, mmaj11, mmaj7b5, 7sg; no block reaches them) stay as the database has them. A test checks every voicing the lookup hands out (notes, bass, fingers, and the chord detector's reading for the common qualities).
- **Guitar chords**: frets and fingers are listed lowest string first; `x` = muted, `0` = open; the first fret shown is 1 when every fretted note is within frets 1–4, else the lowest fretted fret; `position` overrides it but can't be above the lowest fretted note; a finger on the same fret across 2+ strings is a barre, drawn as one bar with the finger number once, on its lowest string; with `labels: intervals` or `notes`, a full barre (over 4+ strings, or over strings fretted higher: F 133211, Bm7 x24232) keeps its bar with every dot's label on it, and a smaller barre (F xx3211, a triad's two strings) draws no bar joining dots that say different things. Frets written without any finger (`F 133211`) still draw their full barres, from `fingerVoicing`'s fingers, with no finger numbers (smaller barres stay unmarked) (#73). A muted string is an x drawn as big and dark as an open string's circle. The chord's root is marked when the tuning is known (6 strings default to standard); an unknown symbol with frets still renders, without root marks.
- **Piano chords**: from a symbol, root position stacked upwards from middle C (C4 = MIDI 60); a slash chord's bass (matched by pitch, not spelling) an octave below; `fingering` maps to keys low to high.
- **Piano voicings** (`pianoVoicing`, `chord` blocks for piano with a symbol; with `pitches` these keys are errors, except `hands`: `left+right` puts the lowest key in the left hand and the others in the right, `left` or `right` every key in that hand): `inversion` (root/first/second/third or 0–3, below the chord's note count; refused on slash chords, shell and rootless) puts the chord's n-th note as tonal lists it lowest, the others stacked above (each the nearest pitch above the one before: C first inversion E4 G4 C5). `octave` (0–8) is the lowest right-hand note's octave (4; 3 with `hands: left`); a slash chord's bass sits an octave below it; keys past the 88 are an error. `voicing`: `close` (default, unchanged from before), `open` (the note above the bass raised an octave: R 5 3, R 5 7 3), `shell` / `shell R-3` (root, third or sus note, seventh or sixth: Cmaj7 C E B; needs a seventh or sixth) or `shell R-7` (root, seventh, third on top: C B E), `rootless A` (3 5 7 9) / `rootless B` (7 9 3 5), dominants with the 13th (or their altered 5th) for the 5th and their altered 9th, sixth chords with the 6 for the 7, half-diminished chords with their b5 and the natural 9th (Dm7b5 A: F Ab C E), dominant sus chords with the 4th for the 3rd and the 5th kept (the 13th when named: G7sus4 A C D F A, B F A C D); for major, minor, dominant (sus4 too) and half-diminished seventh chords and sixth chords (not dim7), and major or minor triads, voiced as their 6/9 chord (C: E G A D, Cm: Eb G A D; the added 6th labeled 6); in a key (chord `key:`, set by progressions from their key), a dominant's unnamed 9th and 13th and a half-diminished chord's unnamed 9th are the key's notes, natural when the key has neither (G7 in C minor F Ab B Eb, E7 in A minor b9 b13, C7 in Eb major 9 b13, Bm7b5 in A minor D F A C), and a dominant whose `resolves-to:` chord (progressions: the next chord) is minor with its root a fifth below takes the b13 in any key; other chords keep the natural 9th; rootless voicings default to octave 3 (lowest note C3–B3, so they stay within C3–C5); without `octave`, a rootless voicing below its low interval limits (`belowLowIntervalLimit`, in piano-chord.ts) takes the other form, then octave 4 (G7sus4 A → F3 A3 C4 D4, A7sus4 → G3 B3 D4 E4), and with `left+right` its right hand starts at F3 or above the same way (C7 A → C2 + Bb3 D4 E4 A4); the added 9th and 13th join the drawn set (lit, spelled from the root, labeled 9 and 13). An inversion (no `title`, no `name`) is titled with its slash name (C/E). No doubled bass: the right hand leaves out the pitch class of the note under it (the left hand's with `left+right`: a slash bass, else the inversion's note for close and open, else the root; or a slash chord's bass) unless the voicing is close and that note is the chord's root or perfect fifth (C both hands C3 + C4 E4 G4; C/G G3 + C4 E4 G4; D/F# without hands F#3 + D4 A4; Cmaj7 shell both hands C3 + E4 B4), or the chord is a triad in close position with `left+right`: the right hand keeps the whole triad in root position over any bass, as method books write it (C/E and C first inversion E3 + C4 E4 G4, D/F# F#3 + D4 F#4 A4; fingers 5 + 1 3 5), while seventh chords keep the rule (C7/E E3 + C4 G4 Bb4); a right hand that would be left empty keeps its notes. A numbered `voicing` stays guitar's and is ignored on piano. `hands: left+right` puts the bass (slash bass, else the inversion's note for close and open voicings, else the root) in the left hand an octave below the right hand's octave, `left` the whole chord in the left hand; each lit key gets `mn-hand-left`/`mn-hand-right`, a hand row past the keys (after the finger row): per hand one bracket under its keys (`mn-hand-bracket`) and one LH / RH label (`mn-key-hand`, `data-mn-keys`) at its middle, the two labels at least 26 px apart (never a per-key L/R, which collided with the R of interval labels), and the hands in the diagram's name. `fingering: auto`: close triads (RH 1 3 5 / 1 2 5 / 1 3 5 by inversion, LH 5 3 1 / 5 3 1 / 5 2 1, a left-hand bass 5; both hands: the right hand's root-position triad 1 3 5 over any bass) and close four-note chords (`tones` 4: sevenths, sixths, add9) by the right hand's spacing, the hand in five-finger position with a second on adjacent fingers: four notes 1 2 3 5, 1 2 4 5 when the top interval is a second (C7 first inversion E G Bb C, C6); three notes (both hands, the third or seventh bass not doubled) top finger 5 for a span of a fifth or more, 4 for a fourth or tritone, else 3, the middle one below the top when the top gap is a second, 2 under a top gap of a fourth or more, else 3 (C7 with both hands: C3 + 1 2 3 5, E3 + G4 Bb4 C5 1 3 4, G3 + 1 2 3 5, Bb3 + C5 E5 G5 1 3 5; Cmaj7 alike; C7/E: E3 + C4 G4 Bb4 1 3 5); the left hand alone mirrored (C7 5 3 2 1 in root position and first and second inversion, 5 4 2 1 in third); a left-hand bass 5; rootless and shell voicings by the same spacing rules, in the hand that plays them: `hands: left` / `right`, else the left hand when the lowest note is below middle C (rootless Dm7 F3 A3 C4 E4: 5 3 2 1; G7sus4 C3 D3 F3 A3: 5 4 2 1), the right hand above it; with both hands a left-hand root 5 and the right hand by its spacing (two notes 1 5 / 1 4 / 1 3 / 1 2 by span); a hand spanning more than an octave (shell R-7) has none; else (open, five notes and more, a slash bass without hands, a rootless or shell hand wider than an octave) an error asking for the fingers. `inversion` keeps its guitar meaning in `voicings:` and `triads:` rows.
- **Note sets** (keyboard, fretboard): from a scale, a chord, or a list; a list with octaves highlights those exact keys, otherwise every octave. A shorthand line is tried as scale, exact notes, chord, then list. The root (scales, chords) gets its own color. Degrees: a chord's interval numbers (1 3 5 7), a scale's positions. A list (`notes:` or a shorthand line) takes a `root:` (a note name, any case, ♯/♭, an octave ignored): each note's interval from it, spelled by letters (D# above C is #2; a unison or octave that isn't perfect takes the usual name: C# is b2, Cb is 7), degrees its interval numbers, the root's color when it's in the list (it needn't be), `highlight:` by interval; the list still plays one note at a time and has no fretboard positions (keyboard fingering: see Keyboard range). With a root, a shorthand line is read as a list first. `root:` with a scale or a chord is an error (they have their own root), and so is a `root:` in a `chord` block, so is a root that isn't a note name; without `root:` a list has no degrees or intervals (labels show names). Interval labels are written the way musicians do (R, b3, 3, #4, b7). `labels: fingers` only exists with a `fingering:` (piano chords, scales and lists on keyboards).
- **Keyboard range**: without `range:`, a scale or mode (pitch classes with a scale type: `scale:`, a shorthand scale, `modes:` rows, `compare:`, `scales-for:`, a chord over a scale) shows two octaves from its tonic to the tonic (`keyRangeOf`, `scaleTonicOf`): the tonic from G3 up to F#4, so the keyboard stays nearest middle C (C major C4–C6, D dorian D4–D6, A minor pentatonic A3–A5, F# major F#4–F#6); only those keys are lit and played (a black-key tonic's white key below, F# major's E#, stays unlit; faint compare and chord marks stay between the tonics too), so playback runs tonic up to tonic and back. Each mode of a `modes:` row gets its own tonic. Chords and lists of pitch classes: two octaves from middle C (C4–B5); exact pitches: the whole octaves around them; a `range:` is kept as written, every octave in it lit. A range never starts or ends on a black key (it widens to the white key next to it: F# major draws F4–G6). A scale's `fingering` is one finger (1–5; anything else is an error) per note of one octave from the root up to the root above (scale size + 1 fingers, else an error), or `auto` / `auto right` / `right` / `auto left` / `left` (`hand` words and parentheses allowed): the standard method-book fingering of a major, harmonic minor, melodic minor (going up: C# and F# take 2 3 1 2 3 4 1 2 in the right hand, else the harmonic minor's) or natural minor (`minor`, `aeolian`: the harmonic minor's) scale (`standardScaleFingering`, tables by tonic pitch class, enharmonic keys alike; other scales, lists and chords refuse `auto`), titled "<scale>, right hand" / "left hand" unless `title` is set. Without a range the keyboard shows the scale's two octaves, tonic to tonic, as without fingering (an octave lower for `auto left`: C major C3–C5), with fingers in both octaves; with one, every octave stays lit and every scale key in it gets a finger (`scaleFingers`): its degree's finger; a root takes the octave's first finger when it is the lowest key, its last when the highest, and between octaves the finger that continues the pattern (`junctionFinger`): the one finger that a hand's step reaches from the 7th degree's finger and that leads to the 2nd degree's (right hand going up: next finger or thumb under; left hand: next finger down or a finger over the thumb; the hand is the one the octave's own steps fit), so Bb major written 2 1 2 3 1 2 3 4 gives A 3, Bb 4, C 1; when no single finger fits, the first and last fingers when equal, else whichever is 1, else the first: C major RH over two octaves 1231234 1231234 5, LH 5432132 1432132 1. A keyboard chord's `fingering` keeps covering one octave from the first root inside the range (else the one below it). With a `labels:` other than fingers, the labels stay on the keys and the fingers get their own row past the keys' near end. A list's `fingering` puts one finger on each note in the order written (G F E D C with 5 4 3 2 1 puts 5 on G): its exact pitches, or for pitch classes each note in the octave nearest the one before (the same note again is the octave above; a tritone goes up), the first in middle C's octave or on its first key inside the range; without a range the keyboard shows those keys, from the lowest to the highest. A number of fingers other than the number of notes, a key listed twice, or a fingered note past the range is an error. `fingering: auto both` (also `both`, `both hands`, `auto (both hands)`; `isBothHandsFingering`) on a keyboard scale draws two keyboards (#49), `columns: 1` unless the block sets `columns`: the same block with `fingering: auto right`, then with `fingering: auto left`, so each is exactly what that fingering draws alone (C major C4–C6 1231234…5, then C3–C5 5432132…1), with every other key kept in both (a `range` too); a `title` becomes "<title>, right hand" / "<title>, left hand". No scale (a list, a chord, exact pitches) or a scale without a standard fingering is one error for the block (the `auto` messages); in a `---` row (or a `modes:` row) a diagram with it is an error in its cell (it can't split in two).
- **Fretboard**: high string on top (tab convention); frets 0–12 by default; up to fret 36. An inlay is left out where a note dot (or a compared scale's ring) would cover part of it. Named tunings (`tuning:`, any case, a trailing `(…)` ignored): standard, drop D, drop C (C G C F A D), drop B (B F# B E G# C#), drop A (A E A D F# B), double drop D (D A D G B D), DADGAD, open G, open D, open E, open C (C G C G C E), open A (E A E A C# E), half step down, whole step down (D G C F A D), modal C6 or C6 (C A C G C E), bass, ukulele (#54); else notes with octaves, or letters.
- **Capo** (`chord` and `fretboard` blocks): `capo: N` (1–12; 0 or `none` is no capo; piano chords refuse it) draws a bar across the strings and a caption "Capo N" under the diagram. Frets count from the capo by default (as players read them): the capo is drawn where the nut is, open strings sound at it, a fretboard's inlays stay on the real neck's frets. `frets-from: nut` (checked even without a capo) counts written frets from the nut: the diagram shows the real neck (the capo on its fret, strings it plays as rings on it; a fretboard shows nothing behind it, frets `capo`–`capo+12` by default); a sounding fret behind the capo, or a fretboard's `frets:` starting behind it, is an error. The chord is the shape as played (named, detected and looked up from frets counted from the capo); it sounds `capo` semitones higher (`soundingChord`: spelled as the new key usually is, C + 1 = Db, Am + 1 = Bbm). Root marks, `labels: notes`, `data-midi`, `data-mn-pitches` and playback use the sounding notes; `data-mn-sounds` holds the sounding chord, and playback's piano redrawing uses it (titled "A (sounds B)"). `sounds: yes` adds it to the caption ("Capo 2, sounds B"); a written chord must be the one the shape sounds (by root, notes and bass), else an error names it; `sounds:` with a chord and no capo is an error. Fretboard strings are named by what they sound; positions are found from the capo as if it were the nut (`frets:` and positions rows count from it; `positions:` frets from the nut add the capo). `capo`, `frets-from` and (chords) `sounds` are shareable in a row; `voicings:`, `triads:` and transitions draw shapes as played from the capo and drop `frets-from`. Relative frets stop at 36 − capo.
- **Fingering in words** (#53): `caption: fingering` on a `chord` block (shareable in rows; reaches `voicings:`, `triads:` and transition rows) writes `fingeringInWords` as a `figcaption.mn-fingering` in a `figure.mn-captioned` around the diagram (HTML, so it wraps and is read; the SVG and its accessible name are unchanged; hidden while the play bar shows the chord redrawn for the other instrument, shown again with its own); `caption: none`/`no`/`off`/`false` writes nothing; any other value is an error. Without the key the output is unchanged. Guitar, in order: the capo (frets count from it, from the nut with `frets-from: nut`), the strings to strum (all, from string n, or from n to m when the top strings don't sound), each finger from the low string up (`Index`, `Middle`, `Ring`, `Pinky`, `Thumb`), a barre said once, at its lowest string (a finger on 2+ strings at one fret, every string between fretted at that fret or higher, or muted: "Index bars the 1st fret, strings 1 to 6", strings from the highest to the lowest number; with an open string or a lower fret between, the strings are listed instead: "Index on the A and G strings, 2nd fret"), a fretted string without a finger as "Press …", muted strings between the lowest and highest sounding ones, open strings ("at the capo" with a capo). Missing fingers come from `fingerVoicing` on the shape played from the capo. Strings: by note name ("the A string") when every name differs or only the two outer strings share one ("the low E string", "the high E string"); otherwise (drop D, DADGAD, open tunings) every string by number and note ("the 4th string (D)"), so no two read alike; no tuning (not 6 strings, none given): numbers. Names are spelled as the tuning is written (`tuningNames`: "half step down" is Eb Ab Db Gb Bb Eb, `Bb1 Eb2 …` keeps its flats), else with sharps. Piano: one sentence per hand (left first; one unnamed hand without `hands`, except a rootless or shell voicing that `fingering: auto` gives to the left hand below middle C, which says "Left hand:"), each finger on its key (`thumb`, `index finger`, `middle finger`, `ring finger`, `little finger`), keys spelled as the chord spells them, every key with its octave when a name comes twice; without fingers, "Play C, E and G." `check --describe` adds a `words:` line to captioned chords only.
- **Several diagrams** (`chord`, `keyboard`, `fretboard`; never `chords`): lines of only `---` (3 dashes or more) separate diagrams; empty parts are skipped. The first part holds shared settings when it has no shorthand line and only shareable keys (`orientation`, `hand`, `labels`; chord: `instrument`, `tuning`, `range`, `capo`, `frets-from`, `sounds`, and the piano voicing keys `voicing`, `inversion`, `octave`, `hands`, `fingering`; keyboard: `range`, `highlight`, `compare`; fretboard: `tuning`, `frets`, `highlight`, `compare`, `position`, `shape`, `strings`, `numbering`) plus `columns` (1–12, a grid of at most that many columns that wraps on narrow screens; only there). A first part with `columns` is always the shared settings: any non-shareable key there is an error naming it. Precedence: the diagram's non-empty key, the shared one, the render options' default (normalized: invalid values ignored, any case), the language default. Shared `orientation` / `hand` are checked once for the block. A block of only separators draws nothing. Diagrams are capped at 12 strings and 88 keys. A diagram with a mistake shows its message in its cell, the others draw, and the block is not ok (Publish keeps its source visible). At most 32 diagrams; shared settings with no diagram are an error.
- **Orientation and hand**: chord diagrams are vertical by default (nut on top, low string on the left); fretboards horizontal (high string on top, nut on the left); keyboards horizontal (low notes on the left); vertical keyboards have their low notes at the bottom and black keys on the left. Piano chords follow the keyboard default. `hand: left` mirrors guitar diagrams and fretboards; keyboards ignore it (the value is still checked). Text is never rotated or mirrored. A title wider than its diagram widens and centers it.
- **Chord names in words**: read wherever a symbol is (`symbol:`, `name:`, the shorthand), the reverse of `parseChord().name` (round trip), any case; with a space the words reading wins (`E 5` is E5), without one the symbol does. The shorthand's chord ends where frets may start (a word starting with x or a digit), at the longest start that reads as a chord. The words stay the title. A chord that doesn't read and has no frets is an error suggesting `A, Am, A7 or A Major`.
- **Generated voicings**: `generateVoicings` and `voicings:` sound every required tone (root, third or sus, sevenths and sixths, altered notes, the highest natural extension; the perfect fifth, lower natural extensions and an 11th chord's major third may be left out), only chord tones, with the bass (a slash chord's, else the inversion's note: the chord's n-th note as tonal lists it; the root by default, any note on re-entrant tunings) lowest by pitch. On three strings or fewer no note is doubled while a tone is missing. No pitch sounds on two strings (a unison: Cmaj7 x3240x, C x35050), except on re-entrant tunings (ukulele F 2010). Fretted notes stay within 4 frets (5 with a stretch, 6 at most), up to fret 15 by default (or the area's last fret); no muted string between sounding ones unless asked, except the known jazz grips (standard tuning, `isJazzGrip`: the Hendrix grip R 3 b7 #9 on the A string x3234x, m7b5 2x221x and dim7 3x232x from the low E, the 13 3x345x), added when they play the chord and ranked with the known shapes, their muted strings free (#72); at least 4 strings (2 for power chords, fewer when fewer are allowed). Fingers (`fingerVoicing`): a search over every split of each fret's notes into single notes and barres (a barre: adjacent strings at one fret, every string under it sounding at that fret or higher; only the index, on the lowest fret, may lie over strings fretted higher; only the index and the ring finger barre, never the middle; the pinky only the two highest strings (standard 6/9 grips: E6/9 022122 is 0-2-3-1-4-4, Cm6/9 x31233), at a higher cost, and never in generated voicings), finger numbers going up with the frets (then from the low string), a finger at most one fret past one fret per finger from another, at most 4; cost: fingers, 2.5 per barre (−2 for an index barre on the lowest fret reaching the top string without lying over higher frets), 0.1 per fret away from one finger per fret, 1.5 for two fingers on one fret three strings apart with a finger between on a higher fret (awkward). Ranking (lower is easier): fingers, span, 0.3 × base fret (lower positions first: Bb x13331 before 688766), barre (1 for E-/A-shape barres and small barres of 2–3 strings, else 3), reach (3 frets apart across 2 strings), awkward fingering, stretch, muted strings (inner 2, high 1.5, low 1; power chords only 1.5 per low string, so they sit on the low strings), outside the usual open chords: open strings far below the frets and open strings ringing inside the shape, a missing fifth; minus 3 for the usual open chords (a fixed list, standard tuning), 2 for E-/A-shape barres, 1.5 for the other CAGED shapes and power chords; ties go to more strings, then lower on the neck. Shapes: power chord, shell (root, third and seventh or sixth: three notes on three strings, the root lowest, x3x45x, 3x34xx; a doubled root or third is not one, #72), triad (3 adjacent strings, 3 notes), open (open strings, 4+ strings, frets 1–4; the top string may be muted: A x0222x, Bb x1033x, #72), then in standard tuning the CAGED shapes (the open C A G E D chord of the same quality slid up by its distance or an octave more, root on that shape's string), named `C shape` … `D shape` with a barre or not (the ranking still favors E and A shapes held with a barre; it counts only these shapes). - **Chord recognition**: with no symbol and no chord `name`, frets (in the block's tuning; 6 strings default to standard) or piano pitches name the chord and give its root marks. Piano pitches written with names spell the name instead: readings from the written names are added, and the spelling cost is the root (2) and bass (1) spelled otherwise plus 1 per chord tone written another way, at most 3 (Db F B Eb is Db9, C# F B D# C#9); a 7#5, 7#5b9 or 7#5#9 reading also gets its b13 twin (7b13, 7b9b13, 7#9b13), so G B F Ab Eb is G7b9b13 and G B F Ab D# G7#5b9. Altered dominants (7b13, 7#5b9, 7#5#9, 7b9b13, 7#9b13, 13b9, 13#11, 7b9#11, 7#9#11) are kept readings. shells: third and seventh only), windows by lowest note: rootless C3–D4 (D3–D4 after the first chord, so a long progression comes back up; with `left+right` the right hand F3–F4), R-7 shell root F2–E3, R-3 shell root Ab2–G3 (each shell window twelve semitones, so every root fits; a chord outside every window takes the nearest); a shell or rootless voicing whose sounding notes (with the hands) have two neighbours below their low interval limit (`belowLowIntervalLimit`, lowest note per interval: m2 E3, M2 Eb3, m3 C3, M3 Bb2, P4 Bb2, TT B2, P5 Bb1, m6 G2, M6 F2, m7 F2, M7 F2, m9 E2, M9 Eb2, m10 C2, M10 Bb1; octave and wider none) is left out while a clear one exists, the first chord's form switching to the other form in its window (rather than an octave up) when it blurs there (Am7 R-3 A2 C3 G3 → R-7 A2 G3 C4; rootless G7sus4 A C3 D3 F3 A3 → B F3 A3 C4 D4; Abm7 Ab2 Gb3 B3; Bbmaj7 Bb2 D3 A3 stays R-3; both hands A2 + C4 G4 stays R-3), all moved by `octave` − 3 octaves; with `hands: left+right` a shell is passed one octave up (root in the left hand where chosen, third and seventh an octave up), and after the first chord its windows are the left hand's root C2–G3 and the right hand's lowest note G3–C5, not the one-hand shell windows, so the guide tones lead and the root goes where they need it (A minor iiø7 V7 i7: B2 + D4 A4, E2 + D4 G#4, A2 + C4 G4; Am7 D7 Gmaj7: A2 + C4 G4, D2 + C4 F#4, G2 + B3 F#4; Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 stays D3 + F4 C5, G2 + F4 B4, C3 + E4 B4); each chord block gets `voicing: <style> <form>`, `octave` and `hands`; a chord that can't take the voicing gets the block's keys (and its error). `voice-leading: yes` with `octave`, or with `hands: left` / `right` for close or open voicings, is an error; a section's piano keyboards share one range (whole octaves around all their keys) unless it would pass 3 octaves or a chord doesn't voice (then each keeps its own). `---` sections each have a key; a first part with only shared keys applies to all.
- **Harmonize a scale** (#43, `harmonize`, `readHarmonyScale`, `progression` with `harmonize:`): any 7-note scale: a key (`readKey`: major, minor, the modes; ionian and aeolian read as major and minor) or a scale tonal knows with 7 notes (`scaleName` aliases: `A natural minor` is A minor; spelled as `scaleSpelling` spells it, G# harmonic minor keeps F##); other note counts, unknown scales, and keys or scales whose key signature would pass 7 sharps or flats are errors naming why (the other spelling, scale type kept). The key its numerals are written in: the key itself for major, minor and the modes, else the major or minor key of the scale's third. One chord per degree, from the scale's own notes: `triads` and `sevenths` stacked in thirds (`stackedChord`; an unnamed stack is left out), `sus` a sus4 when the 4th above is perfect, else a sus2 when the 2nd is major, `power` the root and fifth; sus and power need a perfect fifth, and a degree without one is left out (`skipped`, with the reason; the block's key line is followed by "Left out: degree N (note), reason"). Numerals and Nashville numbers are `numeralOf` with `byDegree` in that key, so each reads back to its chord (`readNumeral`, which reads `bb` and `##` too: an ultralocrian's `bbVII`, one below the minor key's 7th): modes count from the major scale (D dorian i ii bIII IV v vi° bVII), minor keys mark raised 6ths and 7ths except the leading tone's diminished chords (A melodic minor imaj7 ii7 bIIImaj7#5 IV7 V7 #viø7 viiø7). Functions: `harmonicFunction` in that key when it is tonic, subdominant or dominant, else by degree (major T S T S D T D, minor T S T S D S D). Block: `harmonize: triads | sevenths | sus | power` (also triad, seventh, 7th, 7ths, 7, 5), with `key:` required and no chords line (both errors in words); `transpose:` moves the tonic as its major or minor key would, then takes of that spelling and its enharmonic the one that reads with fewer accidentals (B locrian up 4 is D# locrian, not Eb locrian with 8 flats; E phrygian down 1 is D# phrygian, not Eb phrygian); counts 7 chords toward the block's 32; a first part with `harmonize:` but no `key:` is shared by every section (each with its own `key:`), while a first part with both is a harmonized section of its own, whose `harmonize:` doesn't reach the sections after it; an empty `harmonize:` is an error saying what to write, and `harmonize: none` cancels a shared one; everything else (diagrams, numerals, functions, piano keys, play bar) as any progression.
- **Circle of fifths** (#44, fence `circle`): major keys clockwise from C at the top (F#/Gb and D#m/Ebm share a place), minor keys inside, diminished chords (each major key's vii°) innermost, signatures outside; any mode (`key:` or the shorthand, `mode:` when the key has no mode word; a key that has one and a different `mode:` is an error). The key's seven chords sit on their triad's cell (major on its key, minor on its relative major, diminished on the key it leads to), spelled by the key, with their numerals (accidentals from the major scale); a minor or aeolian key's degree 5 is the harmonic minor's V (V7 with sevenths) on the major ring, unless `dominant: minor` (v; other values are an error); the tonic (degree 1) solid in the root color, degrees 4 and 5 solid in the accent (`neighbours: no` turns it off), the others tinted; the signatures of the marked major keys as they're spelled (C# major: 7♯), or the ring's own label when that spelling would pass 7 (G# in C# major shows 4♭); a key past 7 sharps or flats (`Gbm`, `G#`, `A# dorian`) is an error naming the other spelling. Cells and the row of chord chips carry `data-midi` (the root) and `data-mn-strum` (the chord from G3–F#4 up): a click strums them; the key's chords (cells and chips) also carry `data-mn-chroma` (the root's pitch class), and while the bar plays the scale each note lights the key's chord on that root (cell and chip), never a chord outside the key; the bar plays the key's scale from middle C's octave to the tonic above, on piano by default; changing the sound never redraws the wheel (`data-mn-redraw="no"`).
- **App controls on timed play bars** (#77): the `play` option `timedBar(bar)` is called once per bar that plays in time (strum, picking, progression, ChordPro chart), after the bar is built, with `kind` (`strum`, `picking`, `progression`, `chart`), `element` (the `.mn-play`), `drawing`, `compound` (the tempo counts dotted quarters), `tempo()` (the tempo set on the bar, 20–240, what the next pass plays), `onTempo(listener)` (each slider change; returns a function that stops it) and `add(control)` (into a `.mn-play-app` slot made at the first call, the bar's last child, so app controls come last in the focus order and show and hide with the bar; they get the bar's button styles). Its returned function is called on `destroy`; a hook or listener that throws changes nothing in the bar. Without `timedBar`, bars are unchanged.

- **Progressions in time** (#46, `playProgression`, `progressionEvents`, `runProgression`): one chord a bar, or its `beats` (#75: written beats, quarters in 4/4, eighths in 6/8; rounded, 1 at least; the last chord fills its bar; each beat strums the chord sounding then, `bar` also strikes at each chord change, an arpeggio starts again on a new chord and rings to the bar's or the chord's end, a pattern's slots play their beat's chord); `playProgression` chords are symbols or `{ chord, pitches?, beats? }` (pitches from the name when absent); chord symbols take the strum's guitar voicing (`strumVoicing`; a piano voicing, `pianoVoicing`, with `sound: 'piano'`), given notes play as given; `strum`: `beats` (a down strum on each counted beat, by default), `bar` (one strum ringing the bar), `arpeggio` (eighths: the bass, then up the strings and back down, each note ringing to the bar's end), or `pattern` (a strumming pattern, its bars in turn, its time signature, its chords ignored); `beatsPerBar` 1–16 (4) and `beatUnit` (4): compound time counts dotted quarters like strumming patterns; tempo 20–240 counted beats, 90 (60 compound); a metronome click (`kind: 'click'`, on by default) on each counted beat (bar's first 88 / velocity 92, others 84 / 76), quieter than the count-in (one bar of clicks on every written beat, as strum patterns; first pass only); strings 12 ms apart on guitar, a block on piano; a non-looping pass rings 1.2 s; at most 256 bars. - **Play bar of progressions and charts** (#46): with `play`, a `progression` block and a `chords` chart (`[data-mn-progression-play]`) get a bar above the chords, always shown (`.mn-play-static`): play in time, loop, count-in, click (on), tempo (the block's `tempo:`/`bpm:` or the song's `{tempo}` 20–240, else the app's `bpm` in the counted beat), volume, strum (On the beats, Once a bar, Arpeggio, and Pattern when `strum:` is a pattern; a change restarts from bar 1), sound (piano for piano progressions and charts). - **Chart bars** (#75, `lineBeats`, `chartChords`): a lyrics line with a bar line `|` is measured: the chords between two bar lines (a line's text before its first `|` or after its last one counts only with a chord) share the bar, by its beat marks when they add up to the bar's counted beats (every chord with dots: a `.` is one of its beats, `[C]. . . - **Picking patterns** (#51, fence `picking`): `key: value` lines (time, subdivision, swing, chord, chords or progression (one chord a bar, spaces, commas or `|`), tempo or bpm, tuning, title, pattern; others an error) and pattern lines, one token a slot: `p` the thumb on the bass (the grip's lowest sounding string), `p'` the thumb on the alternate bass, `i` `m` `a` the fingers on strings 3 2 1, `1`–`N` a string (1 the highest), `+` or glued letters a pinch (`p+a`, `pa`; one note a string), `-` `.` `_` rests, `|` bar lines; count lines (with `&` or `+`, counts only) skipped; `’` reads as `'`.
