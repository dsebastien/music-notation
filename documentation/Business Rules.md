# Business Rules

- **Block languages**: `chord`, `chords`, `keyboard`, `fretboard` (0.1); `tab` and its alias `score` (0.5); `strum`, `progression`, `circle` (0.8). Fence names are stable API: never rename one.
- **Block syntax**: `key: value` lines, keys case-insensitive, last value wins; an optional first line without a key is the block's shorthand. Any other line without a key is an error, except in `strum` (pattern lines) and `progression` (chord lines, anywhere among the keys).
- **Empty blocks** (only whitespace) render nothing, successfully: templates scaffold empty blocks.
- **Errors**: a block with a mistake renders an inline message (what's wrong and what to write) and never throws. On Publish its source stays visible.
- **Publish**: blocks are rendered once (`data-mp-rendered` on the `<pre>`: `pending`, `done`, `error`); the source `<pre>` is hidden only after a successful render; the page is watched (MutationObserver, one pass per frame) because Publish swaps content as you navigate.
- **Chord symbols**: `♯`/`♭` are read as `#`/`b`; a slash starts a bass only when a note name follows (`C6/9` has no bass). Suffix spellings are mapped to one canonical form (`theory/suffix.ts`: Δ7/M7 → maj7, min6 → m6, ø7 → m7b5, m(maj7) → mMaj7, alt → 7alt…) before tonal and the voicing data see them, so every symbol the parser accepts also draws. Alterations and additions in whole groups of parentheses read like the same symbol without them (`C7(b9)` = C7b9, `C7(b5,#9)` = C7b5#9, `Cm(add9)` = Cmadd9), for the quality name too, after the written suffix (`C-(maj7)` keeps its own name); unbalanced or empty parentheses and commas outside a group are not a chord (`C,`, `C()`); the root and the type reach tonal apart, so a group starting with an accidental never changes the root (`C(#5)` = Caug, `C(b9)` is no chord, not C♭9); chord-sheet spellings tonal knows under another name are mapped (#5 → aug; -5 and (b5) → Mb5 "Major flat 5", maj7b5 → M7b5, mmaj9 → mMaj9, m6/9 and m6add9 → m69 "Minor 6/9", sus2sus4 → sus24). A flat root stays a root: `Cb5` is a C♭ power chord. Roots and basses take a double sharp (`##` or `x`, read as `##`; a root's `x` before a digit is a chord sheet's count, `Ax2`) or a double flat (`bb`), the longest accidental first (`F##dim` is vii° in G# minor; `Cbb5` is a C𝄫 power chord); their names say "Double Sharp" and "Double Flat", and read back. `parseChord` accepts any other chord tonal knows, with its suffix as the quality.
- **Voicing lookup** (`voicings` entry): a symbol's n-th voicing from chords-db; canonical suffixes reach it under its own names (major, minor, mmaj7, mmaj9, maj7b5, alt, 69); a chord it doesn't have (Cmaj9#11, C7b5#9, Csus2sus4…) gets the generator's 4 easiest voicings, cached per symbol (the last 64) and handed out as copies (a block's position changes its base fret).
- **Guitar chords**: frets and fingers are listed lowest string first; `x` = muted, `0` = open; the first fret shown is 1 when every fretted note is within frets 1–4, else the lowest fretted fret; `position` overrides it but can't be above the lowest fretted note; a finger on the same fret across 2+ strings is a barre. The chord's root is marked when the tuning is known (6 strings default to standard); an unknown symbol with frets still renders, without root marks.
- **Piano chords**: from a symbol, root position stacked upwards from middle C (C4 = MIDI 60); a slash chord's bass (matched by pitch, not spelling) an octave below; `fingering` maps to keys low to high.
- **Piano voicings** (`pianoVoicing`, `chord` blocks for piano with a symbol; with `pitches` these keys are errors): `inversion` (root/first/second/third or 0–3, below the chord's note count; refused on slash chords, shell and rootless) puts the chord's n-th note as tonal lists it lowest, the others stacked above (each the nearest pitch above the one before: C first inversion E4 G4 C5). `octave` (0–8) is the lowest right-hand note's octave (4; 3 with `hands: left`); a slash chord's bass sits an octave below it; keys past the 88 are an error. `voicing`: `close` (default, unchanged from before), `open` (the note above the bass raised an octave: R 5 3, R 5 7 3), `shell` / `shell R-3` (root, third or sus note, seventh or sixth: Cmaj7 C E B; needs a seventh or sixth) or `shell R-7` (root, seventh, third on top: C B E), `rootless A` (3 5 7 9) / `rootless B` (7 9 3 5), dominants with the 13th (or their altered 5th) for the 5th and their altered 9th, sixth chords with the 6 for the 7, half-diminished chords with their b5 and the natural 9th (Dm7b5 A: F Ab C E); for major, minor, dominant and half-diminished seventh chords and sixth chords (not dim7); rootless voicings default to octave 3 (lowest note C3–B3, so they stay within C3–C5); the added 9th and 13th join the drawn set (lit, spelled from the root, labeled 9 and 13). An inversion (no `title`, no `name`) is titled with its slash name (C/E). No doubled bass: the right hand leaves out the pitch class of the note under it (the left hand's with `left+right`: a slash bass, else the inversion's note for close and open, else the root; or a slash chord's bass) unless the voicing is close and that note is the chord's root or perfect fifth (C both hands C3 + C4 E4 G4; C/G G3 + C4 E4 G4; D/F# F#3 + D4 A4; Cmaj7 shell both hands C3 + E4 B4); a right hand that would be left empty keeps its notes. A numbered `voicing` stays guitar's and is ignored on piano. `hands: left+right` puts the bass (slash bass, else the inversion's note for close and open voicings, else the root) in the left hand an octave below the right hand's octave, `left` the whole chord in the left hand; each lit key gets `mn-hand-left`/`mn-hand-right`, an L/R row past the keys (after the finger row), and the hands in the diagram's name. `fingering: auto`: close triads only (RH 1 3 5 / 1 2 5 / 1 3 5 by inversion, LH 5 3 1 / 5 3 1 / 5 2 1, a left-hand bass 5), else an error asking for the fingers. `inversion` keeps its guitar meaning in `voicings:` and `triads:` rows.
- **Note sets** (keyboard, fretboard): from a scale, a chord, or a list; a list with octaves highlights those exact keys, otherwise every octave. A shorthand line is tried as scale, exact notes, chord, then list. The root (scales, chords) gets its own color. Degrees: a chord's interval numbers (1 3 5 7), a scale's positions. A list (`notes:` or a shorthand line) takes a `root:` (a note name, any case, ♯/♭, an octave ignored): each note's interval from it, spelled by letters (D# above C is #2; a unison or octave that isn't perfect takes the usual name: C# is b2, Cb is 7), degrees its interval numbers, the root's color when it's in the list (it needn't be), `highlight:` by interval; the list still plays one note at a time and has no fretboard positions (keyboard fingering: see Keyboard range). With a root, a shorthand line is read as a list first. `root:` with a scale or a chord is an error (they have their own root), and so is a `root:` in a `chord` block, so is a root that isn't a note name; without `root:` a list has no degrees or intervals (labels show names). Interval labels are written the way musicians do (R, b3, 3, #4, b7). `labels: fingers` only exists with a `fingering:` (piano chords, scales and lists on keyboards).
- **Keyboard range**: two octaves from middle C by default; whole octaves around exact pitches; a range never starts or ends on a black key. A scale's `fingering` is one finger (1–5; anything else is an error) per note of one octave from the root up to the root above (scale size + 1 fingers, else an error), or `auto` / `auto right` / `right` / `auto left` / `left` (`hand` words and parentheses allowed): the standard method-book fingering of a major or harmonic minor scale (`standardScaleFingering`, tables by tonic pitch class, enharmonic keys alike; other scales, lists and chords refuse `auto`), titled "<scale>, right hand" / "left hand" unless `title` is set. Without a range the keyboard shows one octave, root to root (middle C's; the octave below for `auto left`); with one, every octave stays lit and every scale key in it gets a finger (`scaleFingers`): its degree's finger; a root takes the octave's first finger when it is the lowest key, its last when the highest, and between octaves the thumb side's (the first and last fingers when equal, else whichever is 1, else the first): C major RH over two octaves 1231234 1231234 5, LH 5432132 1432132 1. A keyboard chord's `fingering` keeps covering one octave from the first root inside the range (else the one below it). With a `labels:` other than fingers, the labels stay on the keys and the fingers get their own row past the keys' near end. A list's `fingering` puts one finger on each note in the order written (G F E D C with 5 4 3 2 1 puts 5 on G): its exact pitches, or for pitch classes each note in the octave nearest the one before (the same note again is the octave above; a tritone goes up), the first in middle C's octave or on its first key inside the range; without a range the keyboard shows those keys, from the lowest to the highest. A number of fingers other than the number of notes, a key listed twice, or a fingered note past the range is an error.
- **Fretboard**: high string on top (tab convention); frets 0–12 by default; up to fret 36.
- **Capo** (`chord` and `fretboard` blocks): `capo: N` (1–12; 0 or `none` is no capo; piano chords refuse it) draws a bar across the strings and a caption "Capo N" under the diagram. Frets count from the capo by default (as players read them): the capo is drawn where the nut is, open strings sound at it, a fretboard's inlays stay on the real neck's frets. `frets-from: nut` (checked even without a capo) counts written frets from the nut: the diagram shows the real neck (the capo on its fret, strings it plays as rings on it; a fretboard shows nothing behind it, frets `capo`–`capo+12` by default); a sounding fret behind the capo, or a fretboard's `frets:` starting behind it, is an error. The chord is the shape as played (named, detected and looked up from frets counted from the capo); it sounds `capo` semitones higher (`soundingChord`: spelled as the new key usually is, C + 1 = Db, Am + 1 = Bbm). Root marks, `labels: notes`, `data-midi`, `data-mn-pitches` and playback use the sounding notes; `data-mn-sounds` holds the sounding chord, and playback's piano redrawing uses it (titled "A (sounds B)"). `sounds: yes` adds it to the caption ("Capo 2, sounds B"); a written chord must be the one the shape sounds (by root, notes and bass), else an error names it; `sounds:` with a chord and no capo is an error. Fretboard strings are named by what they sound; positions are found from the capo as if it were the nut (`frets:` and positions rows count from it; `positions:` frets from the nut add the capo). `capo`, `frets-from` and (chords) `sounds` are shareable in a row; `voicings:`, `triads:` and transitions draw shapes as played from the capo and drop `frets-from`. Relative frets stop at 36 − capo.
- **Several diagrams** (`chord`, `keyboard`, `fretboard`; never `chords`): lines of only `---` (3 dashes or more) separate diagrams; empty parts are skipped. The first part holds shared settings when it has no shorthand line and only shareable keys (`orientation`, `hand`, `labels`; chord: `instrument`, `tuning`, `range`, `capo`, `frets-from`, `sounds`, and the piano voicing keys `voicing`, `inversion`, `octave`, `hands`, `fingering`; keyboard: `range`, `highlight`, `compare`; fretboard: `tuning`, `frets`, `highlight`, `compare`, `position`, `shape`, `strings`, `numbering`) plus `columns` (1–12, a grid of at most that many columns that wraps on narrow screens; only there). A first part with `columns` is always the shared settings: any non-shareable key there is an error naming it. Precedence: the diagram's non-empty key, the shared one, the render options' default (normalized: invalid values ignored, any case), the language default. Shared `orientation` / `hand` are checked once for the block. A block of only separators draws nothing. Diagrams are capped at 12 strings and 88 keys. A diagram with a mistake shows its message in its cell, the others draw, and the block is not ok (Publish keeps its source visible). At most 32 diagrams; shared settings with no diagram are an error.
- **Orientation and hand**: chord diagrams are vertical by default (nut on top, low string on the left); fretboards horizontal (high string on top, nut on the left); keyboards horizontal (low notes on the left); vertical keyboards have their low notes at the bottom and black keys on the left. Piano chords follow the keyboard default. `hand: left` mirrors guitar diagrams and fretboards; keyboards ignore it (the value is still checked). Text is never rotated or mirrored. A title wider than its diagram widens and centers it.
- **Chord names in words**: read wherever a symbol is (`symbol:`, `name:`, the shorthand), the reverse of `parseChord().name` (round trip), any case; with a space the words reading wins (`E 5` is E5), without one the symbol does. The shorthand's chord ends where frets may start (a word starting with x or a digit), at the longest start that reads as a chord. The words stay the title. A chord that doesn't read and has no frets is an error suggesting `A, Am, A7 or A Major`.
- **Generated voicings**: `generateVoicings` and `voicings:` sound every required tone (root, third or sus, sevenths and sixths, altered notes, the highest natural extension; the perfect fifth, lower natural extensions and an 11th chord's major third may be left out), only chord tones, with the bass (a slash chord's, else the inversion's note: the chord's n-th note as tonal lists it; the root by default, any note on re-entrant tunings) lowest by pitch. On three strings or fewer no note is doubled while a tone is missing. Fretted notes stay within 4 frets (5 with a stretch, 6 at most), up to fret 15 by default (or the area's last fret); no muted string between sounding ones unless asked; at least 4 strings (2 for power chords, fewer when fewer are allowed). Fingers (`fingerVoicing`): a search over every split of each fret's notes into single notes and barres (a barre: adjacent strings at one fret, every string under it sounding at that fret or higher; only the index, on the lowest fret, may lie over strings fretted higher), finger numbers going up with the frets (then from the low string), a finger at most one fret past one fret per finger from another, at most 4; cost: fingers, 2.5 per barre (−2 for an index barre on the lowest fret reaching the top string without lying over higher frets), 0.1 per fret away from one finger per fret, 1.5 for two fingers on one fret three strings apart with a finger between on a higher fret (awkward). Ranking (lower is easier): fingers, span, 0.15 × base fret, barre (1 for E-/A-shape barres and small barres of 2–3 strings, else 3), reach (3 frets apart across 2 strings), awkward fingering, stretch, muted strings (inner 2, high 1.5, low 1; not for power chords), outside the usual open chords: open strings far below the frets and open strings ringing inside the shape, a missing fifth; minus 3 for the usual open chords (a fixed list, standard tuning), 2 for E-/A-shape barres, 1.5 for the other CAGED shapes and power chords; ties go to more strings, then lower on the neck. Shapes: power chord, shell (R 3 7/6 only, 3–4 strings), triad (3 adjacent strings, 3 notes), open (open strings, top string sounding, 4+ strings, frets 1–4), then in standard tuning the CAGED shapes (the open C A G E D chord of the same quality slid up by its distance or an octave more, root on that shape's string; E and A with a barre are E-/A-shape barres), other barre. Strings in options and blocks count from the highest (1). Up to 8 strings; `voicings:` draws at most 32, refuses frets, fingers, `voicing`, `position`, `base` and piano keys (and frets in the shorthand), titles each diagram with the block's title (or the chord) and the shape, reads `area: 0-…` from fret 1, on 7–8 strings allows one of gaps, stretch and a wide area (over 12 frets) and `inversion: any` only with six strings or fewer, says which filter emptied the list, keeps the last 32 lists, and can't sit inside a `---` row.
- **Triads**: close triads only (three adjacent strings, one chord tone each, within four frets counting open strings as 0), for three-note chords (tonal's members with an interval); ordered by their lowest fret, then inversion; `top` is the highest pitch's interval label. `triads:` takes exactly three adjacent strings; `inversion:` 0–2 (all by default; one, 0 by default, with `key:`); a key's triads come from tonal's major or natural minor key, each the first voicing at or above the previous one's lowest fret (up to fret 20), else the lowest; numerals by degree (I–VII), lowercase for minor and diminished, ° for diminished. Triad rows label dots with intervals unless `labels:` is given.
- **Guitar dot labels**: `labels: fingers` (default), `intervals` (the chord member's interval label), `notes` (its name), `none`; intervals and notes need a chord the library knows and a known tuning (6 strings default to standard); open strings have no label.
- **Transitions**: from a held voicing (its fingers, worked out when absent), the next chord's 30 easiest voicings ranked by movement + 0.4 × difficulty. Movement: an anchor (same string, fret and finger) is free; a guide (same string and finger, another fret at most 3 frets away: further, the finger is lifted) costs 0.5 + 0.1 per fret; any other finger 1 (a barre counts once); plus 0.3 per fret the lowest fretted note moves. Tips: anchors, then guides (one per finger), then "Move your hand up/down N frets" from 2 frets, else "Lift all your fingers together…"; string names say low/high when two strings share a name, and use the string's number when three do. `smoothProgression`: each chord's 12 easiest voicings, plus its first 6 voicings from the `voicings` lookup (when given and the tuning is standard) that the generator also plays (root in the bass, a hand can hold them); the sequence with the least total (2 × shape cost of each chord + each step's movement), by dynamic programming. Shape cost: 0.4 × difficulty, + 0.6 per fret the lowest fretted note sits above the 5th, + 1.5 no fifth, + 1 reach, + 1 open string inside the shape, + 1 highest string muted, + 0.6 per sounding string below five (open shapes exempt), + 0.5 for a shape other than open, E-shape or A-shape barre, − 0.6 for a lookup voicing; power chords skip the last four and take + 0.8 per muted low string past the first. Chord and progression blocks pass the render option `voicings` (the CLI and Publish give the database). Blocks: `from:` (a chord, words or symbol, with optional frets and fingers; else its easiest voicing) with `to:` and `count:` (3 by default, up to 31), or `progression:` (2 to 32 chords, spaces, or commas or bars); `anchors:` / `guides:` mark strings on any guitar diagram. `to:` without `from:` is an error asking for the held chord; transitions on a non-fretted instrument need a tuning; `triads:` and `voicings:` can't be in one block.
- **Playback**: with the `play` render option, every rendered diagram that has notes (`data-mn-pitches`: a guitar chord's sounding strings low to high when the tuning is known, a keyboard's highlighted keys, a fretboard's dots) gets a bar: play/stop, loop, speed only when several notes play (BPM 20–240 for scales and lists, strum 0–200 ms for chords; values outside are clamped, non-numbers fall back to the defaults), volume 0–100 (sliders as tall as their thumb, the track a centered line: no host or browser offset to correct), sound (its opened list in the page's colors; guitar for guitar diagrams and fretboards, piano for keyboards and piano chords by default; choosing a sound first stops what plays; choosing the other instrument draws the same notes for it: the diagram's chord or scale with its exact pitches (so spelling and labels keep their meaning: Gm's Bb, C9's 9), else a list from the notes and the root mark, with the title, the labels that carry over, the orientation between fretboards and keyboards, and the diagram's name for screen readers: a guitar diagram or a fretboard becomes a keyboard, a keyboard a fretboard (frets 0 to 12, or up to 24 for high notes; notes below the low E or past fret 24 are left out, and nothing is redrawn when none is left); choosing the diagram's own instrument brings its drawing back, and a click on a note plays it on either drawing; a `play` option that starts on the other sound shows that drawing from the start). Chords strum low to high and ring 2.5 s; scales and lists play every note drawn (on a redrawing, the notes it shows), lowest to highest then down, top once, one beat a note. A click on a `data-midi` element plays it for 1.5 s. The bar shows on hover, focus or a tap (touch). A loop stops when its diagram leaves the page; Publish stops a rendering it replaces. Players: the light synthesizer (Karplus-Strong guitar from one buffer per pitch, made before the start time; piano from oscillators with a struck envelope; one AudioContext per window, made on the first play), or the app's (`player`, also a function read at each play with the window the diagram is in at that moment, so a diagram a host moved to another window plays there). The Publish bundle turns it on (`play: false` off) and uses `window.musicNotationSoundfontPlayer` when set.
- **Strumming patterns** (`strum`, 0.8): `key: value` lines (time, subdivision, chord, tempo or bpm, tuning, title, pattern; any other key is an error) and pattern lines; a line of counts (with a digit, only counts) is skipped. Slots are one subdivision of the time signature's beat: 8ths (default), 16ths, triplets (x/4 only); in x/8 an 8th is one slot; time 1–16 over 2, 4, 8 or 16, `C`, `cut`. Symbols: D, U, X (muted), any case, and the arrows ↓ ↑ for D and U; `-` `.` `_` rests; `>` after a strum (or before one, at a token's start) accents it; `( )` ghost strums (not nested, no rests inside, never accented); `[chord]` starts a chord on the next slot; `|` a bar line. Placement: one symbol a slot when rests are written or when every part (split at bar lines and line ends) is whole bars; else by the hand's motion (down on the bar's even slots, up on the odd ones; triplets down-up-down each beat; a muted strum at a token's start (after a space or a bar line: `D X D X`) moves like a down strum, one glued after a symbol (`DX`) takes the next slot), each part padded to its bar's end; dashes alternating with strums (`D-D-U`) are read as separators when the symbols don't fill bars. A wrong count says how many slots a bar has and which other subdivision fits. A line whose every token reads as a chord, with one that isn't a lone D, U or X, followed by a pattern line, is a chord line: each chord starts on the first slot at or after its column, or on the rest the motion put in the gap under it; past the line's end is an error. Chords read like symbols or words (words shown as the symbol); unknown ones suggest others. At most 16 bars; rows of at most 16 slots (whole bars). Drawing: counts (1 & 2 &, 1 e & a, 1 & a, 1 2 3 in x/8), arrows, accent marks, an X for muted strums, ghost strums small and in parentheses, chord names over their slot, bar lines; each slot a `g[data-mn-beat]` (index from 0) with `data-mn-stroke` and an invisible box. Playback: tempo is the block's, else the app's `bpm`, else 90 (20–240), counting the time signature's beat; down strums play the chord's strings low to high, up strums the 4 highest high to low (strings ~12 ms apart, less at fast tempos); velocity 90, accents 120, ghosts 40, muted 80 for 40 ms; a strum rings until the next strum (a muted one too) or the pass's end; strums before the first change take `chord:`, else the last change (the pattern repeats), else E minor (022000) on a standard guitar or the open strings on other tunings; chord notes: the `voicings` lookup's first voicing on a standard guitar, else the easiest generated one for the tuning. The bar: play/stop, loop (each pass started on a timer at the previous one's exact end; no ring past a looping pass), count-in (one bar of clicks, first pass only), tempo, volume, sound; changes apply from the next pass. With a player that reports its start (`reportsStart`, or seen calling `onStart` on any bar of the page), passes are counted from the first pass's `onStart` less the player's own start time when under 250 ms (it takes it every pass); a longer wait is a load, after which the player starts at once (a soundfont), so passes are counted from that `onStart` itself: a first play that waits for a soundfont is neither cut nor overlapped by the second pass. A player not known to report is counted from the play call, and from its first pass's `onStart` when that comes before the second pass is asked for (it is then known for later plays). Each strum's slot (`g[data-mn-beat]`) gets `mn-playing` for one slot from the moment its pass is heard, with #41's follower (it waits for reporting players, else 300 ms; stops after the last strum, on stop, or once the drawing leaves the page). `NoteEvent.velocity` (1–127, 100 when absent): the light synthesizer scales the note's level by velocity/100, the soundfont player passes it to alphaTab (clamped 1–127).
- **Following the notes** (#41): while a diagram plays, each note's `data-midi` element on the drawing on the page (the diagram or its redrawing) has `mn-playing` for the length of its note: every element of the pitch on scales and lists (a fretboard may show a pitch twice); an element with `data-mn-chroma` lights for every note of that pitch class instead, and a chord element (`data-mn-strum`) without one never lights; on a strummed chord, strings sharing a pitch light one each, low to high; a clicked note lights only the clicked element. Lights start when the player calls `onStart` (the notes really sound: the light synthesizer at its start time on the audio clock plus the output latency, once the context has resumed; the soundfont player once the soundfont is in and the song started; its fallback's own report, or at once when the fallback doesn't report). The bar waits for `onStart` from players with `reportsStart` or seen calling it before (on any diagram or strum bar of the page); others light from the play call after 300 ms, and again from `onStart` if it comes later (even once that run has ended; never after Stop). One timer at a time, read against the clock; lights end after the last note, on Stop, on a sound switch, and when the drawing leaves the page (no timer kept). Lit notes: a yellow fill (#facc15) with a ring in the text color and dark labels, over root, characteristic, highlighted and passing colors (the rule is last in the stylesheet and outranks every other mark). Tabs and scores: alphaTab ships no style for its cursor, so the library's styles give the bar cursor a light accent tint, the beat line and the sounding beat's notes the accent color (`--mn-accent`, the player bar's), and selections a light accent tint.
- **Tabs and scores**: `tab` / `score` blocks: settings lines first (`staves: tab|score|both`, `file:`, `tracks:`), then alphaTex; `tab` shows tablature and `score` notation by default; what each staff shows is chosen staff by staff once the score loads (alphaTex or file; alphaTab's stave profile stays Default): `tab` → tablature on stringed staves, notation on staves without strings (drums, piano), `score` → notation only, `both` → notation plus tablature on stringed staves; all tracks unless `tracks:` names some. alphaTab is passed in, never bundled; colors come from the page's text color once the block is on the page (any CSS color, normalized to rgba through a canvas; black when unreadable); objects alphaTab checks with `instanceof` (Map, Uint8Array) are made in the block's window (popouts); workers only with a script URL (Publish renders on the main thread: cross-origin workers fail). `renderTab` never throws and its `ready` settles once and never rejects (drawn, failed, or destroyed before drawing). The player (audio, synthesizer, soundfont) starts at the first Play, so a note full of tabs costs nothing until one plays; when playback can't start, the score stays and Play is disabled with the reason. `printTab` prints a drawn score in black on white, whatever the theme. Errors show alphaTab's first error diagnostic with its line (offset by the settings lines) and column. A file the host can't find or can't read, or bytes alphaTab doesn't know, is an error naming it. The player bar (hover/tap): play/pause, stop, loop, metronome, count-in, speed 25–150%, volume, time. Exports: Guitar Pro 7 and MIDI from alphaTex or file bytes; imports: score files → alphaTex (alphaTab's exporter), text tabs → alphaTex (string lines are ≥ 4 dashes with a bar line or a string name (a letter, or a number 1–8 before a bar line), so a line of dashes alone is a rule; named lines count from one, unnamed ones from three (a markdown table rule isn't a tab); a run splits where its first two names come back (systems without a blank line between); every beat an eighth; bar lines and repeat signs `|:` `:|` end a bar; a bar of dashes only is a whole rest, so a blank stave becomes empty bars, not null; a two-digit fret's partner written one column right on another string is the same beat; text after a line's last bar line is an annotation on the system's last beat when it holds a character no tab line holds (a word, `<--`; dashes allowed then: `| repeat --- 4x`, `|<-- let ring`), when it's a repeat count (`|x2`, `| 3x`), or when a space sets it off and it has no `--` (`| let ring`); else it's music (`|--3--`); tuning written when the strings aren't standard guitar; names matching consecutive strings of a standard guitar keep guitar octaves (e B G D → E4 B3 G3 D3; a lone `e` is E4, a lone `E` E2); bass names (E A D G, B E A D G, lowest first) an octave below; numbers are standard guitar strings (1 = E4); without names, 3 strings are a guitar's top three, 4 a bass, 5 a 5-string bass, 7 a 7-string guitar; drum tabs (every line a drum name: HH, C, R, S, T1, FT, BD, Hi-Hat, Snare, Bass…; B, C or F lines with dashes only stay strings) → a percussion track with alphaTab's default articulations: x o X O g f d b # @ * + are hits, o on a hi-hat open, X O accented, g a ghost note, # a cymbal choke; a run splits where a drum line comes back; the first system decides between strings and drums, and only systems of its kind and tuning are read; drum tracks draw as notation in a `tab` block, see below), chord sheets → ChordPro (chord-only lines merged into the next lyric line at their columns; bar lines, repeat marks, counts (2x, x2) and N.C. allowed among the chords; `Intro: G D Em C` → a comment and its chords; section names → comments; square brackets in lyrics become round, since `[x]` is a chord in ChordPro).
- **Tab bundle** (`music-notation.tab.min.js`, web pages): starts once per page; loads alphaTab, its font and soundfont from jsDelivr the first time a page has a `tab` or `score` block (a failed load keeps every source visible; the next page change tries again); renders on the main thread; marks each `<pre>` like the main bundle (`data-mp-rendered`) and hides it only after a successful draw; redraws a block whose source changed in place, frees blocks that left the page (and shows their source again if it stayed); sets `window.musicNotationSoundfontPlayer` (its own hidden alphaTab instance on the first play, the light synthesizer when the soundfont can't load). The hidden instance loads a song with a guitar track (program 25) and a piano track (program 0): alphaTab loads only the presets its song uses, so without the piano track the piano sound played nothing; it plays once the soundfont is loaded and the player is ready (`playerReady`, the presets are loaded then), or 4 s after the soundfont when the player never says so.
- **Chord recognition**: with no symbol and no chord `name`, frets (in the block's tuning; 6 strings default to standard) or piano pitches name the chord and give its root marks. A chord with a third and a seventh but no fifth is read with the fifth assumed (shells: `3x44xx` is Gmaj7). tonal's readings are spelled as symbols are (M, Madd9, m/ma7, 7add6 → 13, m7add6 → m13, a trailing no5 dropped…) and kept only for common and a few extended qualities; ranked by quality (common first) plus a fixed cost for a slash, then usual root spelling (Bb, F#), then a slash bass spelled as the matching chord tone (C7/Bb, not C7/A#), else with the accidentals of the chord's key. Five notes or more that only move by steps (1 or 2 semitones) within an octave (an octave of the lowest note on top allowed) are a hand position, not a chord: no name, no root marks; four-note clusters (Cmaj9/B) stay chords. A given symbol always wins.
- **Scales**: tonal names plus aliases (natural minor, blues = minor blues), any case; a scale name without a root is an error asking for one. The altered scale switches to its superlocrian degrees (1P 2m 3m 4d 5d 6m 7m: G# A B C D E F#) when tonal's (b9 #9 3 #11) need a double accidental; other scales keep one letter per degree (G# harmonic minor's 7th is F##). Characteristic notes (lydian 4A, dorian 6M, mixolydian 7m, phrygian 2m, locrian 5d, minor/aeolian 6m, blues 5d, major blues 3m, harmonic minor 7M, melodic minor 6M 7M, phrygian dominant 2m 3M) are marked by default when the written name has a mode word or blues; `highlight:` overrides (characteristic, none, or interval labels, each of which must be in the scale). The root keeps its color. `compare:` (a type, or a scale with its root): own notes not in the other scale outlined, its notes missing here drawn as ghosts. Symmetric scales (chromatic, whole tone) have no modes.
- **Positions**: boxes (2 notes per string from the n-th note on the low string), CAGED for 7-note scales (`position: n` on a 7-note scale means CAGED; the major or minor pentatonic inside, box n, plus the other notes on the string that keeps them closest to the box's frets, the lower string on a tie; scales with no pentatonic inside use 3NPS), 3NPS (3 per string from degree n). E = 1, D = 2, C = 3, A = 4, G = 5. Each position takes the lowest octave where every note is at fret 0 or above; with `frets:`, the lowest octave inside them (else the lowest). The fretboard shows the position's frets. `positions:` expands into a row (columns and shared keys in a shared part; the block's `frets:` is dropped) in neck order: position 1 in its lowest octave, each next one in the lowest octave whose lowest fret is at or above the previous one's, or in its own lowest octave when that would go past fret 36; each diagram gets its position's frets, and a list (`1 3`) keeps the frets `all` gives them; `numbering: minor` (or `relative minor`) counts a major pentatonic's boxes and 3NPS positions from its relative minor (position 1 is the relative minor's box 1; a row's titles add "(from F#m)", short so they don't widen the diagrams); a single `position:` with `frets:` that no octave of it fits in is an error; on other scales, on CAGED shapes or without a position it is an error that says where it applies; `modes:` too (ionian and aeolian when the type is exactly major or minor); neither can be used inside a `---` row.
- **Arpeggio positions**: `arpeggio:` is `chord:` with interval labels by default (fretboards; a keyboard plays it one note at a time; both in one block is an error), and a chord with `position:`, `shape:` or `positions:` is an arpeggio too (interval labels by default). Its CAGED position n is the chord tones (members with an interval: a slash chord's bass outside the chord is left out) of position n of the root's major scale, natural minor when the chord has a minor third; chord tones outside that scale are added between the position's notes on the string that keeps them closest to its frets (the lower string on a tie). Octaves, `frets:` with a single position, and `positions:` rows (titled "Am7 arpeggio, E shape") follow the scale rules; 3NPS is an error (2NPS was left out: two chord tones per string climb more than an octave per string pair); `numbering: minor` is a scale-only error.
- **Blocks from properties**: an empty chord / fretboard / keyboard block draws `blockFromProperties` of the note's properties when given (an object or a function, read at each render; a failing function draws nothing). Values become one line each (no line breaks, so a value never adds a key); frets and fingers stored as numbers (YAML drops leading zeros) are padded back (6 strings). Reading properties never throws (a failing value draws nothing). The properties function gets the block's element; on Publish the properties are part of the block's fingerprint. Piano from a tag with a `piano` segment unless the host says. `position` 0 or empty is computed; chord shapes (Open…) say nothing for scales.
- **Transposition**: by whole semitones (fractions rounded, invalid values ignored); keys use their usual spelling (F♯ over G♭, D♭ over C♯, B♭m over A♯m). With a known key (a ChordPro `{key}`, a progression's key), chord roots and basses move by the interval between the old and new tonic, by letters (G → F♯: F♯dim → E♯dim, C → B; Em → E♭m: C → C♭; a triple accidental is simplified); without one, chords keep their own spelling (sharps or flats). A key whose tonic is written with a sharp uses sharps, with a flat flats.
- **ChordPro**: unknown directives are ignored; `#` lines are comments; tab and grid sections are shown as written. Parsing is linear in the input (no backtracking regexes on user text).
- **ChordPro chord diagrams**: `{define}` / `{chord}` in ChordPro 6 syntax (`base-fret` 1–24, frets relative to it, `x`/`N`/`-1` muted; fingers 1–9 or A–Z, anything else none, one per string or dropped; `keys` 0–23 semitones above the root; `display`; `copy`/`copyall` from an earlier define, else the voicing lookup; `diagram off`; the older form without keywords, a base fret then two or more frets (`{define: G 1 3 2 0 0 0 3}`, `-` muted; six values alone are a guitar's six frets from the nut, `{define: G 3 2 0 0 0 3}`), reads as base-fret + frets); a mistake is a message at the top of the chart, the rest still draws, and the block is not ok; a `{chord}` with no diagram is a message in place and also makes the block not ok. The chords used row (`{diagrams}`: on/top/right → top, bottom/below, off; else the `chartDiagrams` option; off by default) lists the lyrics' chords (names starting A–G; N.C., %, annotations skipped) by first appearance as the chart prints them (transposed), at most 32; a chord's own define (by name, else by spelling) first, then the voicing lookup (guitar) or root position from C4 (piano). Transposed: a define with no open string moves (down when its lowest fret stays ≥ 1, else up, at most fret 15, same fingers); one with open strings gives way to the lookup; keys move with the root; display names only when not transposed. Capo (row and `{chord}` in place): guitar shapes as played (the printed names), capo bar drawn, sounding at the capo (`data-mn-sounds`); the row's "Capo N" caption only when the row is at the bottom (the chart's meta line, right above a top row, says it already); piano diagrams draw the sounding chord, titled "G (sounds A)" (keys counted from the sounding root). Instrument: `{instrument: piano|keyboard|guitar}` (an extension; any other value, such as ukulele, is ignored without a message), else the render option, else the note's tags, else guitar. `{chord}` draws in place (the song's define of that name so far, else the lookup), transposed only when bracketed.
- **Publish re-rendering**: a block is rendered again when its source changed in place (fingerprint in `data-mp-source`) or its rendering was removed; a broken edit shows the source again. The bundle starts once per page even if loaded twice.
- **Command-line check** (`music-notation check`, the package's `bin`, `dist/cli.js`): renders every `chord`, `chords`, `keyboard`, `fretboard` and `strum` block of Markdown files (files, folders, globs) with `renderBlock` and the voicings, in linkedom (bundled into the CLI only; no other entry loads it), and prints each error as `file:line: language: message` (the opening fence's line). Exit 0 when no block has an error, 1 when one has, 2 on usage errors. Fences: ``` and ~~~ of 3 or more, closed by the same character at least as long; in quotes and callouts (a quote that ends closes its block) and list items; CRLF; front matter skipped. Tab and score blocks: settings lines checked, then skipped (alphaTex needs alphaTab). An empty diagram block in a note with front matter is skipped (properties aren't read: no YAML parser); otherwise empty. `--describe` / `--json`: what each diagram draws, read from its elements (`data-mn-string`, `data-mn-fret`, `data-mn-note`, `data-mn-chord`, `data-mn-set`, `data-mn-frets`, `data-mn-key`).

- **Packaging**: every entry loads in Node and in bundlers (no JSON or CSS imports in `dist/`); types resolve under `bundler`, `node10` and `NodeNext`.
- **Keys and scale finder** (#42): keys read as `G`, `Em`, `F# minor`, `D dorian` (no mode is major) and are spelled by tonal (F major has Bb, F# major has E#). `findScales` lists scales of up to 8 notes (no chromatic) on every pitch class, with up to `maxOutside` (1) notes played outside them; cost = 2 per outside note + type (major, minor 0; dorian, phrygian, lydian, mixolydian, locrian 0.5; major and minor pentatonic and blues 1; harmonic and melodic minor and their usual modes, bebop, diminished, whole tone 1.25; others 3) + tonic (the first chord's root or first note 0, another chord root 0.25, else 0.5) + 0.05 per missing note; a scale is spelled without double accidentals, on the input's own spelling of the tonic when it has one, then with the fewest accidentals. Items are chords when one isn't a plain note name (or when asked), except notes with octaves (`C4 E4 G4`, not all power chords like `A5 D5 E5`), which are notes; a chord that doesn't read gives no scales. `findKey` scores the 24 major and minor keys: 2 per chord in the key (a minor key's harmonic V and vii° count), else 1 for a dominant resolving to the next chord when that one is in the key, 0.5 for a chord of the parallel key, −1 otherwise; 2 more when the first or last chord is the tonic chord, 1 per dominant-to-tonic move; major wins a tie; not clear when the second key is within 2. The other candidates (up to 4, score above 0) are listed by fewest chords outside them, then score (Am F C G: C major before G major). The tonic takes the chords' spelling of it (unless that key would pass 7 sharps or flats: Gbm Cbm Db is in F# minor), else F# and Db major, C#, G#, Eb and Bb minor. `scales-for:` (chords, a single letter is a major chord) or `scales-for-notes:` on keyboards and fretboards draws the best scale, titled with its name, the first 6 items and the outside notes; it refuses `scale`, `chord`, `notes`, `root`, `modes` and a shorthand line; it is resolved before a block is split into a row, so `positions:` draws the found scale's positions (titled by position).
- **Chords and scales** (#48): `scalesForChord` is `findScales` on the chord's notes, only scales on its root, no outside note. `chordsInScale`: on each note of the scale (as `scaleNoteSet` spells it), the triad and seventh chord stacked in thirds for 7-note scales (null otherwise) with the triad's numeral from the tonic (accidentals from the major scale), and the common chords (major, m, 7, m7, maj7, dim, m7b5, dim7, aug, sus2, sus4, 6, m6) whose notes are all in the scale. A keyboard or fretboard with `scale:` and `chord:` draws the scale; the chord's notes in it are chord tones (`mn-tone`, solid), its other notes passing (`mn-passing`, hollow; the root keeps its color), chord notes outside the scale are drawn faint like a compared scale's; the chord tones are named for screen readers. `arpeggio:` with `scale:` reads the same; with `position:` the scale's position is drawn, and `positions:` keeps the chord in every diagram of the row. Without `scale:`, `chord:`/`arpeggio:` with a position is an arpeggio (#20).
- **Progressions** (#9, fence `progression`): chords as roman numerals, Nashville numbers or chord names (a token starting with A–G), on the lines that aren't `key: value` (before, between or after the keys; several lines are one progression, in order) or after `progression:`, split on spaces, commas and bar lines; a token that doesn't read is an error naming it; at most 32 chords. Keys: major or minor (ionian, aeolian read as such; other modes are an error that says to write major or minor; a key whose signature would pass 7 sharps or flats is an error naming the other spelling: `Gbm` → "write F# minor"); numerals need `key:`, chord names without one take `findKey`'s key, shown with up to two other candidates when not clear. Roman: case is the quality, `°`/`o`, `ø`, `+` and chord suffixes after it (no figured bass: `I6` is C6, `V65`/`V43`/`V42`/`I64` are errors saying to write the bass after a slash); accidentals from the major scale; in minor keys an unmarked or flat 3rd, 6th or 7th is the natural minor's (III = bIII), `#` raises it, and a diminished chord on an unmarked 7th takes the raised 7th (vii° = G#dim in A minor); `X/Y` with a roman Y is X in the major key of Y's root (a written V or vii over a numeral is a secondary dominant whatever its chord); `/N` (a degree) is the bass. Nashville: plain numbers are major, `m` or `-` minor; leading digits after the number are written raised (5⁷), read either way. Numerals of chord names: a root or bass on a pitch of the key (a minor key's raised 7th too) takes the key's spelling first (Fdim in F# is vii°, Abdim7 in A minor vii°7), then the degree by letters, a minor key's raised 3rd, 6th, 7th marked `#` (except vii°), a major or dominant chord a fifth above a chord of the key (not in the key or its parallel key) is `V…/x`, a bass is `/N`. Functions: in the key (a minor key's harmonic V and vii° too) by degree (major T S T S D T D; minor T S T S D S S, the raised 7th D), else borrowed (in the parallel key), else secondary dominant, else chromatic. `transpose:` moves the key (usual spelling) and chord names (by the interval between the keys, letters kept: see Transposition). Diagrams: guitar from `smoothProgression` (else the chord block's voicing lookup, else the chord's name and the message in its cell), or piano (`instrument: piano`, or the note's instrument) in root position from middle C, or `voice-leading: yes` (`voiceLead`: close position, the inversion with the least movement, its lowest note F3–G4, the first chord in root position with its mean nearest D4, a slash bass at least a fourth below; movement = `voiceMotion`, a one-to-one assignment of voices, the smaller chord's notes each to its own note of the larger, sorted pairing, the larger's extra notes counting their distance to the nearest; ties: more common tones, then nearer the center). Shell and rootless `voicing` is voice-led unless `voice-leading: no` (`voiceLeadVoicings`): the first chord in its written form (rootless A, shell R-3 by default) at the bottom of its window, each next one in the form (A/B, R-3/R-7) and octave with the least movement (rootless: every note; shells: third and seventh only), windows by lowest note: rootless C3–D4, R-7 shell root F2–E3, R-3 shell root Bb2–G3, all moved by `octave` − 3 octaves; with `hands: left+right` a shell is passed one octave up (root in the left hand where chosen, third and seventh an octave up); each chord block gets `voicing: <style> <form>`, `octave` and `hands`; a chord that can't take the voicing gets the block's keys (and its error). `voice-leading: yes` with `open`, or with `octave`/`hands` for close voicings, is an error; a section's piano keyboards share one range (whole octaves around all their keys) unless it would pass 3 octaves or a chord doesn't voice (then each keeps its own). `---` sections each have a key; a first part with only shared keys applies to all.
- **Circle of fifths** (#44, fence `circle`): major keys clockwise from C at the top (F#/Gb and D#m/Ebm share a place), minor keys inside, diminished chords (each major key's vii°) innermost, signatures outside; any mode (`key:` or the shorthand, `mode:` when the key has no mode word; a key that has one and a different `mode:` is an error). The key's seven chords sit on their triad's cell (major on its key, minor on its relative major, diminished on the key it leads to), spelled by the key, with their numerals (accidentals from the major scale); the tonic (degree 1) solid in the root color, degrees 4 and 5 solid in the accent (`neighbours: no` turns it off), the others tinted; the signatures of the marked major keys as they're spelled (C# major: 7♯), or the ring's own label when that spelling would pass 7 (G# in C# major shows 4♭); a key past 7 sharps or flats (`Gbm`, `G#`, `A# dorian`) is an error naming the other spelling. Cells and the row of chord chips carry `data-midi` (the root) and `data-mn-strum` (the chord from G3–F#4 up): a click strums them; the key's chords (cells and chips) also carry `data-mn-chroma` (the root's pitch class), and while the bar plays the scale each note lights the key's chord on that root (cell and chip), never a chord outside the key; the bar plays the key's scale from middle C's octave to the tonic above, on piano by default; changing the sound never redraws the wheel (`data-mn-redraw="no"`).
- **Publish snippet**: `progression` and `circle` are block languages; sites must list them in `publish.js` for the bundle to load on pages that only have them. `music-notation check` renders and describes them too (the circle: its key and chords).
