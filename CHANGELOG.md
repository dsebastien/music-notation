# Changelog

## 0.19.1 (2026-11-10)

- **Tabs and scores use alphaTab 1.9.0** (was 1.8.4): notes are spaced a little tighter, bar numbers sit at the start of each line, and hammer-on, pull-off and slide labels are placed above the staff. Playback, the bar and Guitar Pro files work as before. Apps that load alphaTab themselves should move to 1.9.0 too (the peer range is now `^1.9.0`).
- Maintenance: newer development tools (ESLint 10, typescript-eslint 8.72), two errors that keep their cause when they are rethrown, and two slow tests that no longer fail CI at random.

## 0.19.0 (2026-11-10)

- **Copy as image** (#11, with #83): a button on the bar under each block copies a PNG of the selected diagram, with its title and captions, to paste in a chat, a slide or a document. Shift+click, or a block with one diagram, copies the whole block (a row, a progression, a chart's diagrams, a strumming or picking pattern). It uses the page's colors at twice the size, and says "Copied". With playback off (`play: false`), each block gets a small bar with only this button, and it copies the whole block. `copy: false` leaves the button out. Not on tabs and scores yet.
- **Export as SVG and PNG** (#11): for apps, `toSvgString(el)` gives a standalone SVG of a diagram or a whole block (styles inlined, laid out as on the page), and `toPng(el)` a PNG. Both take `theme: 'light' | 'dark'`. The playground has Download SVG and Download PNG.
- **Formula captions** (#52): `caption: formula` under a chord, keyboard or fretboard writes what it is: "Am7 (A minor seventh): A C E G. Formula 1 b3 5 b7. Also written Amin7, Ami7, A-7." A scale adds its steps (W+H W W W+H W). `caption: fingering, formula` gives both. Progressions take it too. Names follow `naming`.
- **Root marks you can tell without colors** (#14): `root-mark: shape` draws the root as a square dot (its finger or label still inside), a square ring on an open string, and a small square on its key. Apps and Publish set it for every block with the `rootMark` render option (Publish: `window.musicNotationOptions.rootMark`). Color stays the default.
- **Descriptions for screen readers** (#14): every chord diagram, keyboard and fretboard has a description that screen readers read after its name: the fingering in words and where the root is, the keys and the root, or the notes string by string.
- **Contrast**: two fallback colors are darker, so they pass on dark pages too (the accent and the wrong-answer red). Themes that set their own colors don't change.
- For apps: `withRootMark`, `ROOT_MARKS`, `parseRootMark`, `rootMarkOf`, the type `RootMark`, `chordFormula`, `scaleFormula`, `descriptionOf`, `toSvgString`, `toPng`, and `copy` in the render and play options. A block drawn without playback now ends with the copy bar.

## 0.18.1 (2026-11-10)

- **Fixed: a volume change didn't reach a playing tab or score.** The `/tab` entry is a bundle of its own, so `volumeChanged()` called through the main entry didn't reach a score's bar. Every copy of the library on a page now shares one list of live plays.

## 0.18.0 (2026-11-10)

- **The tab and score bar looks and works like the other bars** (#82): the same buttons and icons (Play, Stop, Loop, Metronome, Count-in), Speed with its value ("100 %"), always shown below the score. Its volume slider is gone: the volume is the app's, as for every bar (in Music Practice, Settings → Playback; on Publish, `play: { volume }`). For apps: `renderTab` takes `volume`, and `volumeChanged()` applies a new one at once.
- **Fingering in words in progression blocks** (#80): `caption: fingering` in a `progression` block writes how to play each chord under its diagram, in the voicing the progression picked (voice-led piano voicings too). Put it before the first `---` for every section.
- **Usual names for two seventh chords** (#78): the minor-major seventh is now written Am(maj7) and `i(maj7)`, and the augmented-major seventh C+maj7 and `bIII+maj7`, as method books write them (harmonize, numerals, Nashville numbers). Progressions still read the old spellings (`imaj7`, `imMaj7`, `bIIImaj7#5`). Blocks that showed AmMaj7 or Cmaj7#5 now show the new names.
- **Chord change tips name strings as the fingering captions do** (#79): in tunings where two strings share a name (drop D), tips say "the 4th string (D)"; a tuning written with flats keeps them ("the Gb string"). For apps: `transition` and `smoothProgression` take `tuningNames`; the tip texts change in those tunings.

## 0.17.0 (2026-11-09)

- **Name a capo chord by its sound** (#88): `sounds: title` titles the diagram with the chord you hear and the shape you play after it. An Em shape with `capo: 3` is titled "Gm (Em shape)", and the caption says "Capo 3". A `title:` you write stays. It works in a row of diagrams too.
- **Volume changes are heard at once** (#85): when an app changes its volume, what plays takes it right away: diagrams, Play all in turn, strumming and picking patterns, progressions and chord charts. Before, a new volume was heard from the next play or loop pass. For apps: call `volumeChanged()` when your volume changes; a `PlayHandle` has `setVolume`.
- **Touch screens** (#86): a tap on a clickable fretboard or keyboard (the pick layer) no longer reaches the page around it, and keyboards with a pick layer are drawn larger, so a black key is at least 24 px wide. On a narrow phone a wide range still fits the page: use a smaller range there.
- **Fixed: solfège "Do major"** (#87): with `naming: solfege`, "Do major" and "Ré minor" were read as the chords Cmajor and Dminor, so a fretboard drew an arpeggio instead of the scale, and `scale: Ré minor` was an error. They read as C major and D minor now. Chord qualities such as `Ré m7` are read as before.
- **Fixed: tabs and scores scrolled the page** (#89): Play on a tab or score bar moved the page by hundreds of pixels. The player no longer scrolls the page.
- **Playground** (#90): examples for every block (tabs and scores too) and Surprise me, play bars on every diagram, note names, Copy link to share a block, and Copy as Markdown.

## 0.16.0 (2026-11-09)

- **Note names in solfège and German** (#12): write `naming: solfege` or `naming: german` in a block, or `{naming: solfege}` in a ChordPro chart. Solfège shows Do Ré Mi Fa Sol La Si with ♯ and ♭; a chord shows a space before its quality (`Ré m7`, `Sol 7`, `Do/Sol`). German shows H for B and B for B♭. It covers the labels on dots and keys, the string names, the titles the library makes, chart chords and their key line, progressions, the circle of fifths, capo captions and the names screen readers hear. Titles you write are never changed.
- **Write notes in any of the three**: `chord: Rém`, `scale: La mineur`, `notes: Do Mi Sol`, `range: Do3-Si4`, `Fa dièse`, `Re` without the accent, and `H7`. Letters are read first: what reads in letters stays as before (`Do7` is still D diminished). A solfège name is read as one in a block with `naming: solfege`, or on a line with other solfège names. `H` is B in a key or a chord value. A bare `B` is B♭ only in a block that says `naming: german` itself; there, write H for B natural. Errors quote what you wrote.
- Apps and Publish set a default with the `naming` render option (Publish: `window.musicNotationOptions.naming`). The default changes how names show, never how a block is read, so existing notes keep their meaning. Letters stay the default.
- Not in this version: tabs and scores (alphaTab draws its own text), `caption: fingering` sentences and the `check` command's output stay in letters; German note words (Fis, Es, Dur, Moll) and lowercase minor keys aren't read.
- For apps: `toLetters`, `noteInNaming`, `chordInNaming`, `shownText`, `withNaming`, `parseNaming`, `namingOf`, `lettersInBlock`, `NAMINGS` and the type `Naming`.

## 0.15.0 (2026-11-08)

- **Answer on the drawing (pick layer)**: an app can turn a fretboard or a keyboard into something you answer on, for drills and quizzes such as "find every G on string 5". Every string at every fret, the open strings too, and every piano key becomes a target. A click picks it, and so does the keyboard: Tab goes to the drawing, the arrows move (on a fretboard they follow the drawing in every orientation and hand; on a keyboard they go up and down in pitch), Home and End go to the ends, and Space or Enter picks. The app gets the string, the fret, the note and the pitch, and marks the pick right (a check) or wrong (a cross) on the drawing. Screen readers hear each target by its place ("String 5, fret 3", "White key 5"), never by its note, so a drill does not give the answer away. It is off by default: blocks in your notes do not change.
- **Hear what you pick**: with sound on, each pick plays its note for 1.5 s on the app's player and volume, on guitar for a fretboard and on piano for a keyboard. A drawing you answer on has no play bar: a click answers, and you hear what you answered.
- For apps: `renderFretboard` and `renderKeyboard` take `pick: true`; `attachPick(svg, { onPick, play })` makes the layer live and returns it (`mark`, `unmark`, `clear`, `focus`, `targets`, `destroy`); `renderBlock` takes `pick: { onPick, play }` on `fretboard` and `keyboard` blocks and returns the live layers in `picks`. `emptyNoteSet()` draws a neck or a keyboard without dots. New types: `Pick`, `PickAt`, `PickLayer`, `PickOptions`, `PickResult`. New colors: `--mn-right`, `--mn-wrong`.

## 0.14.0 (2026-11-05)

- **One play bar per block, under it**: the bar no longer floats over each diagram on hover. Every block with diagrams that play has one bar under it, a block with one diagram too, so nothing covers the dots and keys. It plays the selected diagram: in a row (`---`, `voicings:`, `positions: all`, modes, both hands), the one you clicked last, outlined and named on the bar, the first one at the start. The bar has play / stop, **play all in turn** (each diagram once, with a beat of silence between two; with loop on, it starts again after the last), loop, the speed and the sound. Choosing the other instrument redraws every diagram of the block for it (#58).
- **Click a diagram to hear it**: a click on a chord, keyboard or fretboard diagram plays it, and a second click stops it. A click on a dot or a key still plays only that note. With the keyboard, Tab moves to each diagram: Space or Enter plays it, Escape stops it (#58).
- **No volume on the bars**: the volume is the app's setting (Music Practice: Settings → Playback; Obsidian Publish: `play: { volume }` in `window.musicNotationOptions`). This changes every bar except the tab and score bar, which keeps its look for now (#82) (#58).
- **The other bars move under what they play**: the bars of strumming and picking patterns are under the pattern and always shown (they floated over it on hover). The bars of progressions and ChordPro charts are under the chords (they were above). A progression's bar is the only bar of its block: a click on one of its chord diagrams plays that chord, on the sound the bar chose (#58).
- **Readable speeds**: the sliders show their value, "90 BPM" or "12 ms", instead of the small ♩ and ≋ signs, which sat low and off-center in the bars (#58).
- **The hand cursor on everything you can click**: diagrams, dots, keys and every bar's buttons, lists and sliders, also in Obsidian, whose buttons showed the arrow. Without playback, diagrams keep the arrow (#81).
- For pages and apps that style or script the bars: the diagram bar is `.mn-play.mn-play-static.mn-block-bar`, one per `.mn-block`, under its diagrams and not inside `.mn-playable`; `.mn-playable` now means a playable diagram, a button (`role="button"`, `tabindex="0"`, named by `aria-labelledby`), and the strum and picking wrapper is `.mn-timed`; the slider value is in `.mn-play-value` (also its `aria-valuetext`); `.mn-play-all` and `.mn-play-name` are new; `mn-play-open` (tap to show the bar) is gone, except on the tab bar; no bar but the tab bar has a Volume slider. `PlayOptions.volume` may be a function, read at each play and each pass of a loop. `TimedBar` and the `timedBar` hook work as before.

## 0.13.0 (2026-11-04)

- **Both hands' scale fingering**: `fingering: auto both` on a keyboard scale draws two keyboards, one above the other: the right hand's standard fingering, then the left hand's an octave lower, each titled with its hand and playable on its own. It works for major, natural minor, harmonic minor and melodic minor scales, with `range`, `labels` and `scales-for` (#49).
- **Fingering in plain words**: `caption: fingering` on a `chord` block writes how to play the chord under the diagram: "Strum from the 5th string (A). Index on the D string, 2nd fret. Middle on the G string, 2nd fret." It names barres, the thumb, muted and open strings, and the capo. In standard tuning, strings go by name (the A string, the low E). In a tuning where two strings share a name, such as drop D or DADGAD, every string goes by number and note ("the 4th string (D)"), spelled as the tuning is written. Piano chords say each hand's fingers on their keys: "Left hand: little finger on E3. Right hand: thumb on C4, middle finger on E4, little finger on G4." The words are text, not part of the drawing, so they wrap, and screen readers and search read them. They work in rows (`---`, `voicings:`, `triads:`) and hide while the play bar shows the chord for the other instrument (#53).
- **Harmonize any scale**: a `progression` block with `key:` and `harmonize: triads`, `sevenths`, `sus` or `power`, and no chords line, draws the chord on each degree of the scale with its numeral, diagrams and the play bar. Any 7-note scale works here: major, minor, the modes, harmonic minor, melodic minor and more. A harmonic minor's sevenths are AmMaj7 Bm7b5 Cmaj7#5 Dm7 E7 Fmaj7 G#dim7 (imaj7 iiø7 bIIImaj7#5 iv7 V7 bVImaj7 vii°7). Degrees with no chord of that kind are left out and said: D dorian has no sus chord on B. Several scales go in one block, separated by `---` (#43).
- Numerals with a double flat or a double sharp (`bbVII`, `##iv`) now read as chords. Before, they were not recognized.
- For apps: `harmonize(scale, { kind })` returns the harmonized chords with their numerals, Nashville numbers and functions, and `readHarmonyScale(text)` reads a scale name (or says why not). `HARMONIZE_KINDS` and the types `HarmonizeKind`, `HarmonizeOptions`, `HarmonyScale`, `HarmonizedChord` and `Harmonized` are new. `fingeringInWords(chord)` returns the sentences of a guitar or piano chord's fingering, with the types `GuitarFingering` and `PianoFingering`. `check --describe` prints a `words:` line for captioned chords.

## 0.12.0 (2026-11-04)

- **Chord charts in time, bar by bar**: write bar lines `|` in a chart line to say how long its chords last when the chart plays in time. The chords between two bar lines share the bar. Dots or slashes give each chord its beats: `| [G5]. . [D5]. [A5]. |` is two beats of G5, then one of D5 and one of A5. A bar with no chord holds the chord before it, and a bar of N.C. is a bar of silence. A line without a bar line still plays one chord a bar, and the chart looks the same as before. This changes how some charts play: a chart that already has bar lines, such as a 12-bar blues grid, now plays with those lengths. Before, each chord played for one bar (#75).
- **Tab exercises with a capo**: `exercise:` now works with `capo:`. The tab shows the frets the diagram shows, counted from the capo, and sounds where the capo puts it. With `frets-from: nut`, it shows the real neck's frets. A minor pentatonic `position: 1` with `capo: 2` is a tab on frets 3 to 6, or 5 to 8 from the nut. Before, the block said to leave the capo out (#76).
- **Long titles wrap**: a chord diagram's or a fretboard's title no longer makes it wider. A long title, such as "F#m7b5, Drop 3 (strings 2-4, 6)" or "G shape, sounds Gb (F#)", wraps onto two or three lines. The other diagrams of the block keep the same room above them, so their necks stay in line. A diagram you titled at length is now narrower and a little taller. In an app that draws diagrams at a fixed width, it no longer draws smaller than the others (#74).
- **Larger diagrams on touch screens**: on phones and tablets, chord diagrams and fretboards are a third larger, so each string is easier to tap. They still fit the page (#73).
- For apps: the `play` option takes `timedBar(bar)`, called with each play bar of strumming and picking patterns, progressions and charts. It lets an app add its own controls to the bar (`add`) and read the tempo set on it (`tempo()`, `onTempo`). The type is `TimedBar` (#77). `playProgression` chords take `beats` (and `pitches` may be left out), and the new `onChord` is called as each chord starts, while `onBar` is called on each bar. `chartChords(song, { transpose })` reads a chart's chords and their beats for `playProgression`, as a `ChartChords` (#75). `exerciseTab` takes `capo` (0 to 12) and writes a `\capo` line (#76). `alignTitleLines(diagrams)` gives diagrams you draw one by one the room of their longest title, so their necks line up. `titleLines`, `titleLineWidth`, `MAX_TITLE_LINES`, `TITLE_LINE` and the `TitleLine` type are new too (#74). On touch screens (`pointer: coarse`), the stylesheet zooms chord diagrams and fretboards by 1.34 (#73).

## 0.11.0 (2026-11-04)

- **Play a progression in time**: with playback on, a `progression` block has a bar above its chords. It plays the progression one chord a bar, with a metronome click, a count-in and a loop, so you can practice the changes at tempo or solo over them. Each chord is strummed on the beats by default. The bar can also strum it once a bar, play it as an arpeggio, or play it to the block's strumming pattern. The chord that plays lights up. New keys: `time:`, `tempo:` (or `bpm:`) and `strum:` (#46).
- **Play a ChordPro chart in time**: a `chords` chart has the same bar. Each chord above the lyrics plays for one bar, at the song's `{tempo}` and `{time}`, with its `{define}` shape and the capo. A chord the chart can't read (N.C.) is skipped (#46).
- **Fingerpicking patterns**: the new `picking` block draws a picking pattern as a small tab and plays it over a chord, or one chord a bar. Write PIMA letters (`p` the bass, `p'` the alternate bass on the chord's fifth, `i m a` on strings 3 2 1), string numbers, pinches (`p+a`) and rests, or name a pattern: `travis`, `alternate bass`, `1-2-3-4`. Its bar has a click, off by default. An empty `picking` block in a fingerpicking pattern note draws `picking_pattern` (#51).
- **Scales and arpeggios as tab exercises**: `exercise: up`, `down` or `up-down` in a `fretboard` block with one position draws the run as a tab under the diagram, to play in time with a count-in and the metronome. `rhythm:` is 8ths (the default), triplets or 16ths, and `tempo:` (or `bpm:`) sets the tempo: without it, the app's tempo, else 60. On Publish, the tab bundle draws it (#47).
- **A name for every voicing**: every diagram of a `voicings:` list now has a name. F xx3211 is "E shape (strings 1-4)", and Bb x1033x and A x0222x are "Open": an open chord no longer needs its top string. The other voicings are "Drop 2", "Drop 3", "Hendrix chord", "Open bass", "Open strings" or the strings they sound ("Strings 3-6"). Before, 121 of 643 diagrams in `voicings: 6` lists (12 roots, 9 common chord kinds) had the chord's name alone. The 7#9 grip on the A string is "Hendrix chord" at every fret: E7#9 x7678x was "C shape". Dim, aug and sus2 triads on the four highest strings are "Strings 1-4". The order of the list does not change (#72).
- **The Hendrix grip in 7#9 lists**: `voicings:` for C7#9 now shows the Hendrix grip x3234x. In 0.10.0, the list drew x32340 instead: the same grip with its top string sounding. Bb7#9 now shows x1012x (not x10121 or x10124), and E7#9 x7678x (not x76780). `voicings: all` for A7#9 gains `x-12-11-12-13-x`, and `12-0-11-12-13-0`, its last diagram, falls off (#72).
- **Publish**: add `'picking'` to the block languages in your `publish.js`, so pages with only picking patterns load the bundle. The snippet in the guide has it.
- For apps: `playProgression`, `progressionEvents`, `progressionPitches`, `runProgression`, `runPasses` and `followTimes` play chords in time. `parsePicking`, `formatPicking`, `namedPicking`, `playPicking`, `pickingEvents`, `pickingScore`, `pickingFrets`, `pluckNote`, `renderPicking` and `PICKING_PATTERNS` are new, and so is `PropertyNames.pickingPattern` (`picking_pattern`). `strumEvents` takes a `spread` (the time between strings, 0 for a block). `exerciseTab(...)` returns the alphaTex of a tab exercise for a `tab` block, and the `tab` render option draws an exercise's tab. `voicingShape` names every voicing, and returns null only for an unknown chord (or frets that sound no note). `renderTab`'s result has `speed()`: the speed set on the player bar (0.25 to 1.5, or 1 without a bar). alphaTab's `api.playbackSpeed` gets that speed only once the player starts, so read `speed()` before the first Play. The `check` command renders and describes `picking` blocks and tab exercises.

## 0.10.1 (2026-11-03)

- **Arpeggio and strumming pattern notes on Obsidian Publish**: empty blocks in arpeggio notes, in guitar chord notes (the fretboard) and in strumming pattern notes now draw from the page's properties. They draw the same as in Music Practice 0.11. An arpeggio note's fretboard (a keyboard on piano) draws its chord's arpeggio, in its CAGED `shape` when it has one. A strumming pattern note's `strum` block draws `strum_pattern`, with `time_signature`, `strum_feel`, `chord_symbol` and `bpm_current` as the tempo. The kind of note comes from its type tag (`type/music/guitar/arpeggio`). Before, these blocks stayed empty on Publish (Music Practice #56, #58).
- **Guitar chord notes draw their arpeggio**: this changes what your pages show. The empty `fretboard` block of a guitar chord note now draws the chord's arpeggio, in Obsidian and on Publish, as in Music Practice. Before, it stayed empty. A chord note page you already published can now show a fretboard where it showed nothing (Music Practice #56).
- **Long text tab lines are fast again**: a text tab line with no bar lines converts in linear time again. Since 0.10.0, a palm mute (PM) or slide line over a very long bar got slower with its length: 499 ms for a palm mute line of 50,000 columns. It now takes 64 ms. The output does not change. Real tabs (about 100 columns) were not affected in practice.
- For apps: `blockFromProperties` and `renderBlock` with `properties` draw these notes too, and `renderBlock` now sends an empty `strum` block through the properties. `PropertyNames` has four new names: `strumPattern`, `timeSignature`, `strumFeel` and `bpmCurrent` (`strum_pattern`, `time_signature`, `strum_feel` and `bpm_current` in `DEFAULT_PROPERTY_NAMES`).

## 0.10.0 (2026-11-03)

- **Voicing lists, one diagram per shape**: `voicings:` now draws one diagram per shape: the easiest ones, put in a row up the neck. Before, it drew every voicing, easiest first. A shape with its highest strings muted is the same shape, so it is left out. A `voicings:` block you already wrote can draw other diagrams, in another order. A now gets its D shape `x-x-7-9-10-9` and its C shape (#72).
- **First voicings you can strum**: the first voicing of a chord in the database is now one you can strum. A grip with a muted string inside the shape comes after the others: Bdim starts with x2343x, not x20x01. The exception is the jazz grips that mute the A string under a root on the low E. 37 chords changed their first voicing, so a `chord` block with only a symbol, or a ChordPro chart's `{diagrams}` row, can draw another shape than before (#72).
- **Jazz grips in voicing lists**: the Hendrix grip (C7#9 x3234x), m7b5 (F#m7b5 2x221x) and dim7 (Gdim7 3x232x) from the low E, and the 13 (G13 3x345x), now show up without `gaps: yes`. They mute strings inside the shape, and players use them that way. C7#9, F#m7b5, Gdim7 and Bm7b5 start with them (#72).
- **Shell means three notes**: a diagram is titled "Shell" only when it has three notes: the root lowest, then the third and the seventh or sixth (x3x45x). Cmaj7 x3250x is no longer called a shell (#72).
- **Barres from frets alone**: frets written without fingers now show a full barre (F `133211`). Before, a barre came only from `fingers`. A small barre (`xx3211`) still shows none. A chord diagram you already wrote without fingers can now draw a barre (#73).
- **Single positions get titles**: one position on a fretboard is titled like the diagrams of `positions: all`. `shape: E` on G major is "G major, E shape", and `position: 2` on A minor pentatonic is "A minor pentatonic, box 2". Before, it had no title (#73).
- **Long labels fit their dots**: labels of 3 characters or more (bb7, #11) are smaller, so they fit inside the dot (#73).
- **Strums only, explained**: when a pattern written as strums only, without `-` for the empty slots, leaves whole beats with no strum at the end of the bar, a note under the pattern says how it was read. `D U D U` in triplets shows "Read as D - U D - U, then 2 beats with no strum…". It's a note, not an error. To place the strums yourself, write a symbol for every slot (#73).
- **More tunings**: `tuning:` takes `Drop C`, `Drop B`, `Drop A`, `Double Drop D`, `Open C`, `Open A`, `Whole step down` and `Modal C6` (also `C6`) (#54).
- **Left-handed pages stay left-handed**: a diagram redrawn for guitar keeps the left hand. Before, a keyboard or piano chord redrawn for guitar on a left-handed page came out right-handed (#60).
- **Text tab lines without bar lines**: the converter splits a line with no bar lines into bars of the meter. A 3-bar line was one bar of 13 sixteenths. It is now `4 4 8 8 4 | 8 8 8 8 4 4 | 2 2` (alphaTex durations). In a Nothing Else Matters arrangement, a 21-column bar is now two full 6/8 bars (#71).
- **Hammer-ons, pull-offs and slides in full bars**: a hammer-on, pull-off or slide pair shares one eighth when a bar would overflow. Nothing Else Matters `7-----7p0----` in 6/8 is now `8 8 8 16 16 8 8` (#71).
- **Same width, same beats**: in text tabs, bars of the same width put the same columns on the same beats. `-----0---3---6-5-` was `r8 4 4 8 4`. It is now `r4 4 4 8 8` (#71).
- **Excerpts of a few strings on a full staff**: a text tab excerpt of 2 to 5 neighbouring guitar strings now draws on a six-string staff, each string in its place, without a `\tuning` line. Before, it drew on the strings it showed. Basses and 7-string guitars keep their strings (#71).
- **Text tabs in tab blocks**: a plain-text tab pasted in a `tab` or `score` block (`e|---0---|`) now draws. The block converts it to alphaTex in memory and keeps your text as you wrote it. Before, the block said "No alphaTex data found". When alphaTab can't draw the conversion, the message says "Couldn't draw this text tab" (#71).
- **Tabs on Obsidian Publish after a failed load**: when alphaTab failed to load once, tabs now draw at the next page change. Before, only a network error was tried again: a script that loaded without alphaTab made every later page fail (#38).
- For apps: the `play` render option takes `hand`: the page's hand, for a diagram redrawn for guitar from one that has none, such as a keyboard. `renderBlock` passes its layout hand. Guitar chords and fretboards carry `data-mn-hand`, and chord diagrams carry `data-mn-voicing` (the frets as written: `x02220`, or `x-12-14-14-14-12` past fret 9). `TabSource.fromText` says the block's alphaTex was converted from a plain-text tab.

Swing doesn't work with 6/8 or with triplets, and the block says so. The count-in clicks each 8th: 1 is the strongest, then the other dotted beats. 3/8 still counts 8ths (#65).

- **Every way to play a chord**: `voicings: all` (or `voicings: 6`) in a `chord` block draws every playable voicing of the chord in a row, the easiest first: the open chords you learn first, then E- and A-shape barres, the CAGED shapes and the rest. Each one is fingered and titled with its shape.
- **Narrow them down**: `area: 5-9` (or `open`), `root-string: 5`, `strings: 1-3` (triads on the top strings), `inversion: 1` (or `any`), `stretch: yes`, `open: no`, `gaps: yes`. Works on any tuning up to 8 strings: `tuning: ukulele` gives the usual ukulele shapes.
- For apps: `generateVoicings(chord, options)`, `fingerVoicing(frets)` (fingers for any voicing) and `voicingShape(frets, chord)`.
- `C7alt` (and `Calt`) are read as altered dominant chords.

## 0.3.0 (2026-10-29)

- **Chords in words**: `A Major`, `F Sharp Minor 7`, `C Major over G` work wherever a chord symbol does, and stay the title. A chord the library doesn't recognize says what to write instead.
- **Chords recognized from their notes**: `frets: x02220` alone (or `pitches:`) draws an A chord, named and with its root marked.
- **Scales and modes**: the usual names (`Natural minor`, `Blues`, `Major pentatonic`), the notes that define a mode in their own color, `compare: major` to see what changes, `modes: C major` for every mode of a key.
- **Positions on the neck**: pentatonic boxes, CAGED shapes and three notes per string, one at a time (`position: 2`, `shape: E`, `shape: 3nps`) or all in a row (`positions: all`).
- **Scale fingering** on keyboards: `fingering: 1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5`.
- **Blocks from a note's properties**: an empty block draws what the note's properties say (chord symbol, frets, fingers; scale root, type and shape), in apps and on Obsidian Publish (`window.musicNotationOptions.properties`).
- **Changes to existing blocks**: modes and blues scales now mark their characteristic notes (`highlight: none` turns it off); chord blocks with only frets or pitches now get a title and root marks.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-28)

- Several diagrams in one block: separate them with `---` to draw a song's chords, a progression or a scale's positions side by side. Settings shared by every diagram go first; `columns: 3` draws a grid. A mistake in one diagram no longer hides the others.
- `orientation: horizontal` turns guitar chords sideways (high string on top, as in tabs); `orientation: vertical` stands fretboards up (nut on top) and draws keyboards with their low notes at the bottom.

- Long titles no longer get cut off on narrow diagrams.
- The 12th-fret double dot shows as two dots on 4-string fretboards (bass, ukulele).

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-28)

First release: music notation from small text blocks, as SVG and HTML.

- `chord`: guitar chord diagrams (frets, fingers, barres, position, root marks, any tuning), about 2,000 voicings to pick from by symbol, and piano chords from a symbol or pitches, with fingering.
- `keyboard`: a piano keyboard with the notes of a scale, a chord or a list; labels as notes, degrees or intervals.
- `fretboard`: a guitar neck with the notes of a scale, a chord or a list, in any tuning.
- `chords`: ChordPro charts (chords over lyrics, sections, comments), transposable.
- A browser bundle for Obsidian Publish that draws every block on the page.
